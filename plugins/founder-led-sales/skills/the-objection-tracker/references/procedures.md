# Procedures: The Objection Tracker

Three procedures, written as instructions to the AI running them on a member's
real material.

**Attribution rule applies here too: paraphrase only, never quotation marks,
name the book.** Cards and sources are in `bank.md`.

Run on what the member brought. Do not ask first and do not read the
situation back; the reply opens with the verdict, and every gap becomes a
question in SEND ME THESE at the end. See Step 1 of `SKILL.md`. Never stop in
prose to ask.

---

## Routing

| What the member brought | Run | Bank sections |
|---|---|---|
| The same doubt keeps appearing across deals, and they cannot tell which doubts matter | The stack | A, B, G, H |
| One live objection they keep losing to, currently answered with argument, with its stack row | The precedent answer | C, D, E, G, H |
| One meeting this week that matters more than the rest | The rehearsal | F, D, G, H |

**If they have never built the stack, run it first even if they asked for an
answer.** An answer to a doubt that does not kill deals is a month spent
building proof nobody was waiting for. Say why in one line and run it. A
stack exists only if the member can paste its row for this doubt: heard,
closed with it, died with it, raised by. A sentence saying it only shows up
in dead deals is not a row.

**When two rows match in one message, run the one the other depends on:** the
stack before the answer, the answer before the rehearsal. Exception: a
meeting inside 48 hours runs the rehearsal.

**A price objection stays here when the buyer's words compare the price to
something they already pay for**; the precedent answer runs. When the words
carry the number alone, return the Refusal Card with WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT
naming the skill that holds your price and this deal's cost to deliver as the
first thing to do.

**Never run more than one in a pass, and never name the one that did not
run.** What the next run needs is asked for in SEND ME THESE, as facts, never
as the name of a procedure.

**Read only the sections routed.** Section G is routed for every procedure,
because what counts as an objection is decided on every run.

---

# 0. The Refusal Card

**Read this before any procedure.** It is what a refusal returns, and it is
also what a missing input returns. A stop with no card is the fifth outcome
this skill does not have.

```
CANNOT ANSWER THIS YET

WHAT YOU BROUGHT
<The objection, the conversations, or the meeting, in one line, with the
count.>

WHY IT WILL NOT HOLD
<The specific defect. Two lines at most. For a missing input this reads
"cannot run yet: <the input or inputs> missing".>

WHAT I WOULD BUILD INSTEAD
<The narrower thing that can be built today. If none survives, write
"none survives" and say what has to change about the offer, or, in the
missing-input form, what has to be brought.>

SEND ME THESE
<Numbered. Every fact this run needed and did not get, as questions they
can answer in one line each, specific to what they wrote, one per
conversation where needed. Three to eight, all at once, never one at a
time.>

WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT
<One line, plain words: what runs next once they answer, or what has to
happen first. Ends with: Send these back and I will redo the table.>
```

Do not apologise and do not soften. No praise line.

**The card is total or it is not a card.** When some conversations are missing
their outcome, build the stack only if ten or more still carry one, and list
the rest under the header. When the member reports the conversations as
counts and themes rather than a list, build the stack on the counts as
reported, mark it provisional on the header, and ask for the exact words,
the signer and the outcome per conversation in SEND ME THESE. The card
returns only when fewer than ten conversations carry an outcome at all, and
then SEND ME THESE asks for the missing outcomes.

**After the card, the output block shrinks to two lines:** STANDS ON and NO CASE
LIKE YOURS ON RECORD. After a missing-input card, STANDS ON reads: no
card; the refusal is the count. It never reads on the doubt the card set
aside, and it never cites a section that was not routed.

# 1. The stack

## What it is

Honest framing: the library does not present this as a named method. It holds
one line, from the Jobs material, that objections arrive in a sequence and the
next one appears the moment the current one is satisfied, and it holds the
rule that adjectives are not evidence. Step 2 is the library's own
distinction. Steps 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 are this skill's rendering. The
counting rule in step 3, that a doubt appearing in a closed deal is noise, is
the skill's and has no founder precedent; bank item H1 says so, and it is
the whole value of the procedure.

## When to run it

The member has ten or more sales conversations with the outcome marked, at
least one closed and one died, and the same doubt keeps appearing.

## What to ask for before you start

1. The exact words of every objection heard across the last ten or more
   sales conversations. Their words, not the member's summary.
2. Against each conversation, whether the deal closed or died.
3. For each objection, who said it: the signer, or someone who cannot sign.

If fewer than ten conversations carry an outcome, return the Refusal Card. If
the objections come as the member's labels or as counts rather than the
buyer's words per conversation, run provisionally: build the stack on what
was reported, mark it provisional, and ask for the words in SEND ME THESE.
Without the words there is no way to be sure a doubt that kills deals is not
two doubts wearing one label.

## Steps

1. **Group the objections into distinct doubts.** Name each group in the
   buyer's words, not the member's. Two objections are the same doubt only if
   a stranger reading both would expect the same thing to go wrong.

2. **Order the doubts by the stage they appear at.** Read bank section B.
   Early doubts are about whether the member will still exist. Later ones are
   about whether they are the right choice. The library's line is that
   satisfying one moves the buyer straight to the next.

3. **Count, for each doubt, the deals that closed with it raised and the deals
   that died with it raised.** A doubt that appears in closed deals is noise:
   buyers say it and buy anyway. A doubt that appears only in dead deals is
   live. This count is the finding. Read bank section G for what counts as a
   real objection. Politeness and stalls stay on the sheet with the verdict
   not an objection, so the outcome count still reconciles; they are never
   built against.

4. **Mark any doubt heard exactly once as unproven.** Do not build against
   it. Wait for the second occurrence.

5. **Only when a doubt is live, write at most two doubts not yet heard.**
   Each is the next rung of the library's one sequence, survival, stability,
   strategy, growth, applied to the member as a vendor, and its "why" is a
   plain clause drawn from the member's situation, never a taxonomy word. A
   doubt already heard once stays in HEARD ALREADY as unproven and is not
   repeated here. For each, name the proof that would answer it, not the
   argument, and the date the member could have it. If no doubt is live, omit
   the section.

6. **Name who raised each doubt only where the message says.** A doubt
   raised only by people who cannot sign is a doubt about the wrong room, not
   about the product; its action is the booking question, with the date it
   goes on the form.

7. **Name the one proof to start building this week, in DO THIS WEEK.** One,
   for the top live doubt, with a day. If the member asked what to say, write
   the provisional script in SAY THIS ON THE NEXT CALL as well.

## Artifact

```
WHAT BUYERS SAID, BY OUTCOME
Calls: <n>    Closed: <n>    Died: <n>
<Provisional: counts as you reported them; exact words per call not yet
logged. Omit this line when the words were logged.>
<Not counted, outcome unknown: <n> (<names>). Omit when zero.>

HEARD ALREADY
  <doubt, their words>  |  heard: <n, or the range their message supports>  |  closed: <n>  |  died: <n>
                         |  said by: <owner / manager, only as the message says>
                         |  verdict: <live / noise / unproven / not an objection,
                                      with "provisional" first when built on counts>

NOT HEARD YET, BUT COMING
  <the doubt>  |  why: <one plain clause from their own situation>  |  proof: <what>  |  by: <date>
  <at most two unless they asked for more; omit the section when no
  doubt is live>
```

Phrasings the message joined with "or" are one row carrying both phrasings.
The died column sums to the reported deaths and the closed column to the
reported closes; "mostly" is a range, never the whole count. Each doubt
appears in exactly one section: a doubt heard once stays in HEARD ALREADY as
unproven and never enters NOT HEARD YET. The "said by" line is omitted when
the message does not say who spoke. Columns that are zero on every row are
omitted. Every cell the skill writes is one line, under fifteen words, not
counting the label, the row scaffolding, or the buyer's own words. Reasoning
goes in WHAT IS WRONG WITH THIS, once. The proof to build this week is not
repeated on the artifact; it lives in DO THIS WEEK.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to run on fewer than ten conversations with the outcome marked.**
Return the Refusal Card, and ask in SEND ME THESE for the outcome of the
unmarked conversations or the conversations that bring the count to ten.
Objections given as the member's labels or as counts are not a refusal; the
stack runs provisionally and asks for the words.

**Refuses to build proof against a doubt heard once.** That is not a refusal
of the run; the doubt is marked unproven on the sheet and the run continues.

> Receipt: Every time Apple satisfied one doubt, the market moved straight to
> the next. Survival, then whether it was a stable business, then product
> strategy, then growth. Jobs decided to get ahead of the sequence instead of
> resenting it. (*Make Something Wonderful*, the Steve Jobs Archive)

# 2. The precedent answer

## What it is

Honest framing: the library states the mechanism plainly, once. Morita, told
nobody would buy a player that could not record, pointed at the millions of
car stereos already sold that also recorded nothing. Steps 2 and 5 are the
library's own instructions in substance. Steps 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 are this
skill's rendering of Morita, Ogilvy and the Jobs restatement rule into a
sequence. The two-part form of the answer is this skill's, not Morita's.

## When to run it

The member has a live objection, with its stack row, that they keep losing to
and are currently answering with argument.

## What to ask for before you start

1. The objection in the buyer's exact words.
2. The member's current answer, word for word.
3. One market or category the buyer already accepts without question, and
   pays into.
4. The stack row for this doubt: heard, closed with it, died with it, raised
   by. If there is no row, the stack runs first.
5. One customer, by name, who bought over this doubt, or the word none.

If any is missing, return the Refusal Card in its missing-input form, naming
every missing item once.

## Steps

1. **Restate the objection in the buyer's own words before writing any
   answer.** Read bank section D. If the restatement is longer than what the
   buyer said, the objection has not been understood yet. Try again.

2. **Find a market that already does the thing the objection says is
   impossible or unacceptable.** Read bank section C. It has to be one the
   buyer already accepts and pays into, not one the member would have to
   defend. Morita's car stereos were already in the buyer's own car.

3. **Test the precedent against the obvious rebuttal.** Write the sentence
   the buyer would say: that is different because. If they can say it and be
   right, it is not a precedent. Find another among the markets the member
   named or the buyer's spend they described. Never place a market on the
   artifact that the member has not confirmed the buyer pays for. You may
   name one candidate only inside SEND ME THESE, as a question the
   member answers yes or no. If no precedent survives, the precedent does not
   exist, and the card says so.

4. **Write the answer as one sentence with two parts: the doubt as restated,
   then the market.** If the buyer's objection is itself two parts, restate
   only the part the precedent answers; the restatement is one part however
   many verbs it has. Any third part, a reason, a benefit, a guarantee, a
   number about the member, is an argument, and the buyer can take an
   argument apart.

5. **Replace every adjective in the answer with a number or a name, or cut
   it.** Read bank section E. Log each swap on the artifact. Never invent the
   number; if none was supplied, the adjective is cut.

6. **Add the third-party version.** The named customer saying it rather than
   the member. If the member named none, the line reads: no customer named;
   the question I will ask the next one who signs, on <day>: <the question>.
   Never a bracketed placeholder for a name.

7. **Say the answer out loud once.** If there is a pause to explain it, it is
   too long.

## Artifact

```
THE ANSWER — <the objection, two words>

THE COUNT FOR THIS DOUBT
  heard: <n>  |  closed with it: <n>  |  died with it: <n>  |  raised by: <who>

THE OBJECTION, THEIR WORDS
  <exactly as the buyer said it, however long>

MY RESTATEMENT — no longer than theirs
  <the restatement>

THE PRECEDENT
  Market that already does this:   <which, confirmed by them>
  Why the buyer already accepts it: <one line>
  The rebuttal:                     that is different because <what>
  Survives it:                      <yes — if no, this sheet is not issued and the run stops instead>

THE ANSWER — one sentence, two parts
  <the sentence>

ADJECTIVES REPLACED
  <adjective>  ->  <the number or name it became, or: cut, no figure supplied>

THIRD-PARTY VERSION
  <customer>  |  their words: <what they said>
  or: no customer named; the question I will ask the next one who signs, on <day>: <the question>
```

The buyer's words, the restatement, the answer and the third-party line run as
long as the buyer's words allow; they are exempt from the fifteen-word rule.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to manufacture a precedent.** If no market the buyer already pays
into does the thing, the precedent does not exist. Return the Refusal Card.
WHAT I WOULD BUILD INSTEAD names the proof that would have to exist before an
answer can, and hands to the skill that builds the demonstration.

**Refuses to run without the stack row.** The stack runs first.

**Refuses to write an answer to an objection that has not been restated in
the buyer's own words first.** Restate, then answer.

> Receipt: Told that nobody would buy a player that could not record, Morita
> pointed at the millions of car stereos already sold that also recorded
> nothing. The buyer already owned the precedent. (*Made in Japan*, Akio Morita)

# 3. The rehearsal

## What it is

Honest framing: the library holds the ratio and the conviction rule, and
nothing else about rehearsing a sales conversation. The ratio is Gallo's
figure for elite speakers, roughly ninety hours of rehearsal per hour on
stage, and the record of Jobs turning up four hours late to an interview
because he was still practising. The conviction rule is assembled from Knight
and Gallo. Duveen's stand-in rehearsal is the one sales case. Steps 1, 2, 4
and 6 are this skill's rendering. Step 3 is Duveen's in substance. Step 5 is
this skill's use of Gallo's ratio. Step 7 is the library's.

## When to run it

The member has one meeting this week that matters more than the rest, and
the decision they want out of it is named.

## What to ask for before you start

1. The meeting, the buyer, and the single decision the member wants out of
   it.
2. The stack and the precedent answers, if they exist. If they do not, say
   in one line that the rehearsal runs weaker without them, and run it.
3. Whether the three branches have been run out loud yet. If yes: the time
   of each, the hours spent so far, and whether the ask came out without a
   flinch. If no: say so; the run returns BRANCHES and THE ASK filled, with
   CUT, RATIO and CONVICTION marked not yet run, and DO THIS WEEK is the
   out-loud run itself, with a day.

## Steps

1. **Write the three branches the conversation can take after the opening
   and before the ask.** Name each branch by a sentence a buyer who could
   sign has actually said, from the stack or the member's log. If none
   exists, the branch reads: not yet heard, and the only branch to rehearse
   is the ask.

2. **For each branch, write the member's next move in one sentence.** A
   move, not a script.

3. **Run all three branches out loud, with someone playing the buyer, and
   time each one.** Read bank section F. If nobody is available, the member
   plays both parts, and the artifact says so. If this has not happened yet,
   the cells read not yet run.

4. **Mark every point where the member reached for a slide instead of a
   proof, and every point where they explained instead of showed.** Cut both.
   Write what replaces them.

5. **Count rehearsal hours against meeting length and write the ratio down,
   even when it embarrasses.** The library's benchmark is on the artifact for
   comparison, and it is a benchmark for a stage, not a sales call. Say so.

6. **Write the ask as one sentence and rehearse that sentence alone five
   more times.**

7. **Run the conviction check.** If the member flinches saying the ask out
   loud, the problem is the offer, not the delivery. Stop. Return the Refusal
   Card with "none survives" and the sentence that could not be said.

## Artifact

```
THE REHEARSAL — <meeting>, <date>

THE DECISION I WANT
  <one sentence>

BRANCHES
  1  they say: <a sentence a signer has said, or: not yet heard>  |  my move: <one sentence>  |  <time, or: not yet run>
  2  ...
  3  ...

CUT
  reached for a slide at: <where, or: not yet run>       ->  replaced with: <the proof>
  explained instead of showed at: <where, or: not yet run> -> replaced with: <the demonstration>

RATIO
  rehearsal hours: <n>  |  meeting: <n>  |  ratio: <n> to 1, or: not yet run
  (the benchmark on record is roughly ninety to one, and it is for a stage,
  not a sales call)

THE ASK — rehearsed five times alone
  <one sentence>

CONVICTION
  said without flinching: <yes / no / not yet said aloud>
```

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to polish a pitch the founder cannot say with conviction.** The
library reads flat delivery as a signal about the offer, not the delivery.
When the check fails, return the Refusal Card: WHAT I WOULD BUILD INSTEAD reads
"none survives", SEND ME THESE asks what would have to change about the
offer for that sentence to be true, and WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT sends them back
to the offer, not to this skill.

**Does not refuse a rehearsal that has not been run out loud yet.** The
out-loud fields are marked not yet run, and DO THIS WEEK is the run.

> Receipt: Gallo's benchmark for elite speakers is roughly ninety hours of
> rehearsal for every hour on stage. Jobs once arrived four hours late to an
> interview because he was still practising. (*The Presentation Secrets of Steve
> Jobs*, Carmine Gallo)

## Notes for all three procedures

**The artifact is the deliverable, and there is always one.** WHAT BUYERS
SAID, BY OUTCOME, THE ANSWER, THE REHEARSAL, or THE REFUSAL CARD. No fifth
outcome on a run. An argument turn re-issues the contested row or line; see
SKILL.md.

**A doubt in a closed deal is noise.** This is the rule the whole skill turns
on. Buyers say things and buy anyway. Only the doubts that appear exclusively
in dead deals get proof built against them.

**Politeness is not evidence.** Read bank section G on every run. Adjectives
with no number, I think, I would, probably, definitely, aspiration presented
as fact: none of it is an objection and none of it is a yes. On the stack it
carries the verdict not an objection.

**STANDS ON cites the card that changed a verdict in this run.** Never a card
that merely agrees. If no card moved a verdict, because every verdict on the
sheet is the count, write: no card moved a verdict; the count did, which is
this skill's rule and not a founder's. Then the nearest card, named as the
nearest. STANDS ON names a case, never a method; the two-part form of the
answer is this skill's.

**The negative-space line names a numbered item in bank section H.** Nearest
if none fits, and say it is the nearest. Stated as an absence, never a
verdict.

**Every cell the skill writes is one line, under fifteen words, not counting
the label, the row scaffolding, or the buyer's own words.** Exempt: THE
OBJECTION, THEIR WORDS, MY RESTATEMENT, THE ANSWER, and the third-party line,
which run as long as the buyer's words allow.

**WHAT KILLS DEALS and SEND ME THESE take the procedure's shape.** On the
stack: the count of live doubts, and questions for the exact words, the
signer and the outcome per conversation. On the answer: the doubt answered
with its row, and questions for the current answer word for word and a
customer who bought over it. On the rehearsal: the branch most likely to end
the meeting, and questions for the times, hours and the flinch.

**SEND ME THESE closes every run.** Three to eight
numbered questions, specific to the member's message, one line each to
answer. Every gap marked on the artifact is a question here. Then the
sentence to type when they have the answers.

**Length.** The whole reply, opening sentence, artifact and block together,
is under 250 words plus 30 per doubt or branch. Every line in the block is an
instruction or a fact; never "consider", "might", "could", "may".

**STANDS ON is two sentences and supports the live doubt.** The case, then
"Here that means:" with the member's doubt named. When the verdict came from
the count alone, sentence one is "This comes from your own [n] calls" and
sentence two names the closest case and how it differs. A case that only
fits a side row is not cited.

**The member's direct question is answered.** When they asked what to say,
SAY THIS ON THE NEXT CALL carries the provisional reply above the
questions. Questions refine a script; they never replace it.

**Nothing on the artifact is inferred.** Who spoke, a date, a count the
message did not give: left off and asked once at the end.

**No internal names in the reply.** The member never sees a procedure name, a
bank section letter, an item code, or another skill's slug. Say the count,
the answer, the rehearsal, the skill that builds the demonstration. Never
name the procedure that did not run. The labels on the artifact are
capitalised; that is the one exception to the voice rule on capitals.

**Never praise the pitch.** Report the count of live doubts and stop.
