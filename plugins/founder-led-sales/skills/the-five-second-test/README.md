# The Five Second Test

Part of **Founder Led Sales**. Version 3.1.0

You have a claim about your product that you believe and your buyers do not.
They take the meeting, say it sounds interesting, and go quiet.

This is not an argument problem. An argument is the wrong instrument. This
skill takes the one claim your business rests on and turns it into something a
buyer can watch happen in under a minute, with their own input, that could
visibly fail and does not.

## What you get back

A proof sheet. It carries the claim you brought and the claim you will
actually prove, with the hedges struck out and the swap visible. The single
variable the demonstration turns on. What the buyer chooses, brings, picks or
drives. The sequence the buyer watches with elapsed time on each moment. The
specific way it could visibly fail. What the buyer is holding when it ends.
Who runs it. And the one sentence you say in the room before they leave.

Built from things you can find this week without spending money.

## Every reply ends with questions

Whatever it gives you back, the last thing on the page is a short numbered
list: everything it needed and did not have, asked in one go, each answerable
in a line. Answer them and re-run it, and the next version is built on facts
instead of gaps. It never asks one question at a time.

## What it refuses to do

It will not build a proof for a claim that needs a hedge to be true. That
includes hedges that do not look like hedges. Nine out of ten is a hedge with
a number on it, because the buyer watches one instance. Within two hours is a
hedge on time, because the buyer cannot watch it now. When the honest sentence
for the one instance they will watch needs "probably", the skill hands the
hedge back, names the narrower claim it would prove instead, and asks one
question.

It will not accept a demonstration where you supply the input. That is a
description with props. And it will not accept one that cannot fail, because a
guaranteed outcome is a performance.

It will not send a demo to the room design just because a demo exists. If you
filled the bottle yourself, there is no proof yet.

It will not cite the iPhone unveiling or the Pepsi Challenge. Neither is in its
sources.

And it will not tell you your demo is good. There is no praise line.

## Where it comes from

Thirty-five verified demonstrations, most of them ones nobody volunteers.

Royce stood a full glass of water on a Silver Ghost running at 1,600 rpm and
not a drop spilled. Michelin built a carousel at the 1895 Paris exposition
with one seat on an air tyre and one on a bare wooden wheel, and the president
of France tried it. Then they staged their own race and seeded the course with
nails so every rider punctured in front of the crowd. Edwin Land rented a hotel
room facing the sun, put a goldfish bowl on the sill where the glare washed it
out, and handed each guest a filter. Estée Lauder was told to leave her
products behind and refused, insisting on five minutes to apply them on the
owner's own face. Gaston Glock shipped agencies a thousand free pistols with a
note to send a cheque if they wanted to keep them. Nine in ten sent the cheque.

And the ones from this decade: Jonathan Ross stopped explaining that Groq was
fast and let anyone type their own question. Tony Xu and three co-founders put
a delivery service live in 43 minutes with a $9 domain and a phone number that
rang all four of them. Scott Wu launched Cognition with its failure rate
published next to the demo. Tobi Lütke watched a demo that passed on an
engineer's laptop fail on a buyer's phone on a train.

Every one is traced to the book or the interview it came from. The skill will
not tell you anything about these people that is not in its own files, and it
does not search the web.

It also carries a list of what those files hold no case of. Among them: no
demonstration that failed in front of a buyer, anywhere in two hundred files,
because the library is written by winners. And no agency deliverable ever
demonstrated to a client. When your situation touches one of those, the skill
says so.

## Two situations it handles

**Nothing has been shown yet**, or what was shown ran on your input rather than
the buyer's. The skill builds the demonstration.

**A real proof exists and still does not land.** The skill stops working on the
proof and designs the room around it — the venue, the alternative in the room,
and the sentence you say on the day the advantage does not show.

## Install

You need the marketplace added once. In Claude Code:

```bash
/plugin marketplace add socialspire/founder-tools
```

Then install the course:

```bash
/plugin install founder-led-sales@socialspire
```

Start a new conversation and type what is actually happening:

> they keep saying it sounds interesting and then going quiet

You do not need to name the skill. If it does not fire, say `prove this claim`
and paste it.

## What to bring

Your actual claim, in your words, and the three sentences you actually say to
support it. Half a sentence is fine. Dictated is fine.

Bring a hypothetical and you get a hypothetical back, which is worth nothing.

## If it gets something wrong

Tell us what you typed and what came back. An invented number, a founder who
never did the thing, a famous demo that should not be there, or a verdict that
went soft when it should have held.
