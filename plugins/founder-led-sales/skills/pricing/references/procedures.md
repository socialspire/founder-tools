# Procedures: Pricing

Two procedures, written as instructions to the AI running them on a member's
real material.

**Attribution rule applies here too: paraphrase only, never quotation marks,
name the book.** Cards and sources are in `bank.md`.

Run on what the member brought. Do not ask first and do not read the
situation back; the reply opens with the ruling, and every gap becomes a
question in SEND ME THESE at the end. See Step 1 of `SKILL.md`. Never stop in
prose to ask.

---

## Routing

| What the member brought | Run | Bank sections |
|---|---|---|
| A deal where price has been raised and they have not yet responded, and the objection is money | The one counter | A, B, D, F |
| A buyer whose objection is timing, cash timing or risk rather than the number, and the member is about to discount | The term swap | C, E, D, F |

**Classify before routing.** Read bank section E. Five classes. Only no money
goes to the one counter. Does not believe the claim goes to the skill that
builds the demonstration, not here. Cannot pay now, no urgency and too much
risk go to the term swap.

**When the buyer's words name two classes, classify by the clause that
carries the condition on saying yes.** A number qualified by proof, time or
risk takes that class, not money. The other class is named to the member in
plain words inside WRONG IF, as the event that would move the ruling.

**Never run both in one pass, and never name the one that did not run.**
What the other would need is asked for in SEND ME THESE, as facts.

**Read only the sections routed.**

---

# 0. The Refusal Card

**Read this before either procedure.** It is what a refusal returns, and it is
also what a missing input returns. A stop with no card is the fourth outcome
this skill does not have.

```
CANNOT RULE ON THIS YET

WHAT YOU BROUGHT
<The deal, the price, and the buyer's words, in one line.>

WHY IT WILL NOT HOLD
<The specific defect. Two lines at most. For a missing input this reads
"cannot run yet: <the input or inputs> missing".>

WHAT I WOULD RULE ON INSTEAD
<The narrower thing that can be ruled on today, if any. Never a ruling
that needs the missing input: without cost there is no walk-away, so
no walk and no accept can be ruled. If none survives, write "none
survives" and say what has to happen first.>

SEND ME THESE
<Numbered. Every fact this run needed and did not get, as questions they
can answer in one line each, specific to this deal. Three to eight, all at
once, never one at a time.>

WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT
<One line, plain words: what runs next once they answer, or what has to
happen first. Ends with: Send these back and I will redo the numbers.>
```

Do not apologise and do not soften. No praise line.

**After the card, the output block shrinks to two lines:** STANDS ON and NO CASE
LIKE YOURS ON RECORD. On a refusal for disbelief in the claim, STANDS ON
names no card: write that the classification is this skill's own and point at
the negative-space line. On a missing-input refusal, STANDS ON cites the card
behind the refusal condition, from a routed section only.

# 1. The one counter

## What it is

Honest framing: the library states this one as a procedure, once, and it is
rare in the corpus for that. Tom Murphy asked the seller what they thought it
was worth. If fair, he accepted outright. If high, he gave one best price. If
refused, he walked. He never won an auction. Steps 3, 4, 5 and 6 are that
procedure mirrored to the selling side. Murphy was the buyer, and the library
holds no seller who ran this loop; bank section F says so. Steps 1, 2 and 7
are this skill's rendering, and the cost-to-deliver requirement in step 1 is
drawn from the cost cards in section B rather than from Murphy.

## When to run it

The member has a deal where price has been raised, the objection is money,
and they have not yet responded.

## What to ask for before you start

1. The asking price.
2. **The cost to deliver this specific deal**: materials, hours at a real
   hourly cost the member would pay someone else, and any third-party cost.
   Not a company-wide average margin. This deal.
3. The buyer's exact words about the price.
4. Whether the buyer has been asked what they think it is worth, or
   volunteered a number unasked, and the number.
5. **The walk-away**: the price below which the member would rather not have
   the deal, decided by the member, and whether it was written before the
   buyer's number was read. The skill never picks it and never derives it
   from cost.

If item 2 or item 5 is missing, return the Refusal Card. Both are the
member's numbers, and a walk-away invented in the room is the buyer's number
with the member's name on it.

## Steps

1. **Compute gross margin on this deal at the asking price**, from the
   cost-to-deliver number. Read bank section B. If the member offers a
   company average instead, the card fires. Carnegie was the only steelmaker
   who knew his cost per ton; a founder who cannot say what one delivery
   costs is not holding a price, they are guessing at one.

2. **Record the member's walk-away in the member's own terms.** Told
   themselves before the email: stated before their number. Wrote it down:
   written before their number. Decided after reading the buyer's number:
   say so on the card; the number stands, but the member should know whose
   it is. Never ask again in SEND ME THESE for a fact the card carries. If the
   member has none, the Refusal Card fires and SEND ME THESE asks for that one
   number, decided now, before they reread the buyer's text.

3. **Record the buyer's number.** Asked and answered: their number. Volunteered
   unasked: their number, marked volunteered, and the run continues; do not
   send the member to ask a question the buyer has already answered. No
   number at all: the run stops at that line of the card. This is a stop, not
   a refusal. Pricing Card is returned with THE RULING and OUTCOME
   marked not reached and the full output block, THE NUMBER THAT HOLDS is
   the walk-away, and DO THIS WEEK is the question to the buyer. Murphy asked
   first, every time.

4. **If their number is at or above the walk-away, accept it outright and
   stop negotiating.** Record it. Grinding past a fair number is the dance
   Murphy refused, and the one Zeckendorf's rule forbids.

5. **If it is below, give exactly one counter, at the walk-away number,
   stated plainly as the only one.** Read bank section A. A counter above the
   walk-away leaves room, and room is the buyer's argument for the second
   counter this skill does not give.

6. **If the counter is refused, walk.** Write the date walked and where the
   freed hours go. If the buyer answers the counter with a number of their
   own, that number goes through step 4, not step 5: at or above the
   walk-away, accept it outright and stop; below it, walk. The one-counter
   rule forbids a second number from the member. It does not forbid taking a
   fair one from the buyer.

7. **Write the message, and the two lines around it.** The message is
   complete: a greeting with [Name], the number said as the only number with
   the sentence that says there is not another after it, the one reason it
   holds with no cost figure in it, the next step with a date, a sign-off.
   When the member named a cash deadline, the next step is a deposit or full
   payment at signature; payment timing and scope are the two levers that
   move instead of the number. Then, when other proposals are open, the
   follow-up to each written out complete and dated.
   Then the single sentence to send if the buyer says no. On a later run,
   record the outcome and the count of consecutive walks on this same
   objection; three in a row is a signal about the offer, not about the
   buyers, and the deal goes to the skill that logs objections with the last
   ten conversations marked closed or died.

## Artifact

```
THE NUMBERS ON THIS DEAL — <deal>, <date>

THE NUMBERS
  asking:                  <n>
  cost to deliver this:    <n>   (<materials> + <hours> at <rate> + <third party>)
  gross margin at asking:  <n>%
  walk-away:               <n>   (<stated / written> before their number, from your words)
  gap to their number:     <n> above / below the walk-away
  gap to their authority:  <n> under what they can sign   (only when the message gives it)

THEIR NUMBER
  <asked and answered / volunteered / not yet named — if not yet named, the run stops here>: <n>

THE RULING
  <at or above walk-away: accept outright, stop negotiating>
  <below: one number, <walk-away>, said as the only one; if refused, walk>
  <not reached, if no number yet>

PRIOR OUTCOMES  (only when a prior outcome on this objection exists)
  deals lost to this exact objection in a row: <n>  (at three, the price is wrong, not the buyer)
```

Every cell is one line, under fifteen words, not counting the label or the
cost breakdown in parentheses. Reasoning goes in WHAT IS WRONG WITH THIS,
once. A first run carries no PRIOR OUTCOMES block. Every dollar difference
quoted after the card is copied from a gap row.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to rule on a price without a cost-to-deliver number for this
specific deal.** Not a company-wide average, not last year's margin, not a
figure an accountant certified for the year. The materials, the hours at a
real rate, and the third-party cost of this one job. Return the Refusal Card.
SEND ME THESE is that one figure.

**Refuses to write the walk-away or the counter for the member.** Both are the
member's numbers. Without a walk-away, the Refusal Card.

**Refuses to produce a second counter.** One, at the walk-away, then walk. If
the member wants a second, the artifact records the first as refused and the
ruling is walk.

## Stop condition

**The buyer has not been asked what they think it is worth and has not
volunteered a number.** This returns the Pricing Card, not the Refusal
Card, stopped at THEIR NUMBER, with the full output block. DO THIS WEEK is
the question.

> Receipt: Tom Murphy asked the seller what they thought it was worth. If fair,
> he accepted outright. If high, he gave one best price. If refused, he walked.
> He never used investment bankers and never won an auction. (*The Outsiders*,
> William Thorndike)

# 2. The term swap

## What it is

Honest framing: the library holds the two anchors as cases, not as a method.
Duveen, told a million and a half was too high, did not move the price; he
left the pieces in the buyer's house for a year on option, and the buyer paid
the full price the day before it expired. Lauder, told the counters were
locked up by exclusives, pitched a bath oil on consignment so the buyer risked
nothing. Steps 3, 4 and 5 are those cases in substance. Steps 1, 2, 6, 7 and
8 are this skill's rendering, and the five classes in step 1 are the skill's.

## When to run it

The buyer's objection is timing, cash timing or risk rather than the number,
and the member is about to discount.

## What to ask for before you start

1. The price.
2. The buyer's exact objection.
3. What the buyer would have to do to say yes today.
4. Three things the member could give that cost something other than margin.
5. **The cost to deliver this specific deal**, as for the counter. Without it
   the swap cannot be priced.
6. Who else quotes on the member's behalf, or that no one does.

If any of the six is missing, return the Refusal Card in its missing-input
form, naming every missing item once.

## Steps

1. **Classify the objection into one of five.** Read bank section E. No
   money. Cannot pay now. Does not believe the claim. No urgency. Too much
   risk. Only the first is about price, and it goes to the counter.

2. **If the buyer does not believe the claim, do not touch the price.**
   Consignment, options and pilots hand a doubter free inventory and a reason
   to delay, and nothing is on record either way; see section F.
   Return the Refusal Card: WHAT I WOULD RULE ON INSTEAD names the claim the
   buyer does not believe, and WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT sends them to the skill
   that builds the demonstration.

3. **If the buyer cannot pay now, hold the number and sell them time.** Read
   bank section C. A dated, non-refundable deposit against the full price, or
   payment timing, the way Duveen offered to help with cash and never with a
   discount. The number does not move at any date.

4. **If it is no urgency, hold the number and move the clock.** Possession
   before payment with a real expiry, the way Duveen left the pieces in the
   house for a year. Or a longer decision window at the same price. The
   expiry is held.

5. **If it is too much risk, hold the number and carry the risk.** Lauder's
   consignment. Foroughi's guaranteed floor. A paid pilot at full rate. A
   stated condition under which the member refunds. The price does not move;
   the risk does.

6. **Price the swap.** What it costs the member in cash and in hours if the
   buyer never converts, from the cost to deliver. Written on the card.

7. **Set the floor at the asking price, the date through which it holds, and
   name who else quotes on the member's behalf.** On a swap the floor is the
   asking price; there is no lower number, because a lower number is the
   counter's business and the counter is not running. Everyone who quotes has
   to know it, or one of them will go under it.

8. **Use exactly one swap on this deal.** Two concessions teach the buyer
   that a third exists.

## Artifact

```
THE TERMS ON THIS DEAL — <deal>, <date>

THE OBJECTION, CLASSIFIED
  their words:  <the clause that names the objection; the full sentence is
                 in the situation read>
  what it really is: <no money / cannot pay now / does not believe the claim /
                 no urgency / too much risk>
  <no money: this is a price conversation, not a terms one>
  <does not believe the claim: not a price problem; it needs a
   demonstration first; stop here>

THE TERM YOU GIVE INSTEAD — exactly one
  kind:        <time / risk>
  what it is:  <one line>
  real expiry: <date, held>
  cost to me if they never convert: <cash> / <hours>

PRICE AFTER THAT TERM
  <n>   (equals the asking price, or it is a discount)

THE FLOOR EVERYONE QUOTES
  price: <the asking price, no lower>   held through: <that term's expiry>
  who else quotes for you: <names, or no one>

TERMS GIVEN ON THIS DEAL
  <n> of 1
```

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to swap terms when the buyer does not believe the claim.** A doubter
given free inventory has a reason to delay and nothing to decide. The library
holds no case of it either way; see section F. Return the Refusal Card and
route to the demonstration.

**Refuses to price a swap without this deal's cost to deliver.** Missing-input
form.

**Refuses to approve more than one concession per deal.**

**Refuses to let the price after the swap differ from the asking price.** If
it does, the swap is a discount wearing a different name.

> Receipt: When Rockefeller Jr. said a million and a half was too high, Duveen
> did not drop the number. He left the pieces in Rockefeller's house for a year
> on option. Rockefeller paid the full price the day before it expired. (*The
> Days of Duveen*, S.N. Behrman)

## Notes for both procedures

**The artifact is the deliverable, and there is always one.** THE NUMBERS ON THIS DEAL,
THE TERMS ON THIS DEAL, or THE REFUSAL CARD. No fourth outcome on a run. An argument
turn re-issues the contested line; see SKILL.md.

**This skill holds a number that already exists.** It does not set one, and it
does not write the walk-away or the counter. Setting a price is a different
job, and the reply says so in plain words when a member asks.

**Cost is this deal's cost.** A company-wide margin is not a cost to deliver.
The refusal fires on the substitution every time, in both procedures.

**STANDS ON cites the card that changed a ruling in this run.** If Murphy's
procedure drove the counter, it is Murphy. If Duveen's option drove the swap,
it is Duveen. Never a card that merely agrees. On a disbelief refusal, no
card: say the classification is this skill's and point at the negative-space
line. STANDS ON names the case, never a method; the counter loop is this
skill's mirror of Murphy, and the reply says so when asked.

**The negative-space line names a numbered item in bank section F.** Nearest
if none fits, and say it is the nearest. Stated as an absence, never a
verdict.

**Every cell is one line, under fifteen words, not counting the label, the
cost breakdown in parentheses, or the buyer's own words.** The buyer's words
cell carries the clause that names the objection; the full sentence lives in
the situation read.

**Length.** The whole reply, opening sentence, card and block together, is
under 380 words. One deal, one card. Every line in the block is an
instruction or a fact; never "consider", "might", "could", "may".

**STANDS ON is two sentences.** The case, with the seat named when it is the
other seat, then "Here that means:" with this deal named. A case that cannot
be mapped in one sentence is not cited.

**The cost never reaches the buyer.** It lives on the card. A message that
carries the cost figure is a defect.

**Nothing on the card is inferred.** A fact the message does not give is left
off and asked once at the end; a fact the card carries is never asked.

**SEND ME THESE closes every run.** Three to eight
numbered questions, specific to this deal, one line each to answer: the
buyer's exact words, whether the walk-away was written before their number,
who else quotes, the cost lines, whether the buyer doubts the product or the
payoff. Every gap marked unknown on the card is a question here. Then the
sentence to type when they have the answers.

**No internal names in the reply.** The member never sees a procedure name, a
bank section letter, or another skill's slug. Say the number, the terms, the
skill that builds the demonstration. Never name the procedure that did not
run. The labels on the card are capitalised; that is the one exception to
the voice rule on capitals.

**Never praise the price.** Report the margin and the walk-away and stop.
