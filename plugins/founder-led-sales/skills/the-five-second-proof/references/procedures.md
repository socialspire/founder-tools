# Procedures: The Five-Second Proof

Two procedures, written as instructions to the AI running them on a member's
real material.

**Attribution rule applies here too: paraphrase only, never quotation marks,
name the book.** Cards and sources are in `bank.md`.

Run on what the member brought. Do not ask first and do not read the
situation back; the reply opens with the verdict, and every gap becomes a
question in SEND ME THESE at the end. See Step 1 of `SKILL.md`. Never stop in
prose to ask.

---

## Routing

| What the member brought | Run | Bank sections |
|---|---|---|
| A claim buyers do not act on. Interest, then silence. A deck that is not landing. Nothing has ever been shown, or what has been shown fails the proof gate below. | The Five-Second Proof | A, B, C, E, F, G, I, plus H if they sell a service or software or the product is not built |
| A demonstration that passes the proof gate and still does not land. A buyer environment that flattens an advantage that has already been shown. | The Rigged Room | D, C, G, I |

**The proof gate runs first.** A demonstration counts as a proof only if all
three hold: the buyer chooses the input, the outcome can visibly fail, and it
proves the same advantage the room would be built around. A demo run on
material the founder prepared is a description with props. If the existing
demo fails any part of the gate, no proof exists yet: build the proof, and say
in WHAT IS WRONG WITH THIS that the demo carried no risk.

**Never run both in one pass, and never name the one that did not run.** When
the second would be required later, state the condition in the member's own
words as the last line of SEND ME THESE: if a buyer runs this on their own file
and still stalls, send me the call notes.

**Read only the sections routed.** The bank is thirty-five cards and the member
is waiting.

---

# 0. The Refusal Card

**Read this before either procedure.** It is what a refusal returns, and it is
also what a missing input returns. A stop with no card is the fourth outcome
this skill does not have.

```
CANNOT BUILD THIS YET

WHAT YOU BROUGHT
<Their claim or their advantage, in their words, one line.>

WHY IT WILL NOT HOLD IN FRONT OF A BUYER
<The specific defect. Two lines at most. For a missing input this reads
"cannot run yet: <the input> is missing".>

THE NARROWER CLAIM I WOULD PROVE INSTEAD
<The half that is unconditionally true, or your best reading of their
claim from what they gave. If none survives, write "none survives" and
say what has to change about the offer.>

SEND ME THESE
<Numbered. Every fact this run needed and did not get, as questions they
can answer in one line each, specific to what they wrote. Three to eight,
all at once, never one at a time.>

WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT
<One line, plain words, saying what happens once they answer.>
```

Do not apologise and do not soften. No praise line. After the card, the
output block shrinks to two lines: STANDS ON and NO CASE LIKE YOURS ON RECORD. The card carries the questions.

# 1. The Five-Second Proof

## What it is

Honest framing: the library does not present this as a named or numbered
method. It presents thirty-five separate demonstrations and the action
statements drawn from them. Steps 2, 3, 4 and 6 below are the library's own
action statements in substance. Steps 1 and 5 are this skill's rendering: the
hedge strike, and naming the failure, which the bank draws from Royce testing
parts to destruction and Carnegie inspecting harder than the buyer's test,
neither of which is an action statement about demonstrations. Read the whole
thing as an assembly of the material rather than as a procedure any founder
wrote down.

The under-a-minute promise is also the skill's. Bank section I, item 5.

## When to run it

The member has a claim buyers do not act on, or a demonstration that fails the
proof gate. Also run it when they ask for help with a deck, because the deck
is usually the problem.

## What to ask for before you start

1. The single biggest claim about the product, in one sentence, with the
   number in it if there is one.
2. The three sentences they currently use to support that claim in a sales
   conversation, word for word. Reconstructed from the last real conversation
   is acceptable. Invented is not.

If the claim is missing, return the Refusal Card in its missing-input form.
If the three sentences are missing, run on the claim, mark them on the
artifact as not supplied, and ask for them in SEND ME THESE.

## Steps

1. **Write the claim back and strike every hedge.** Remove "typically", "in
   most cases", "for the right customer", "up to", "can". Then strike two
   kinds of hedge that do not look like hedges. **A rate is a hedge.** Nine
   out of ten, 94 percent, most, on average: these pass the word list and fail
   the rule, because the buyer watches one instance and one in ten it does not
   happen. **A deferred result is a hedge on time.** Within two hours, by the
   third, in thirty days: the buyer cannot watch it now. Rewrite the claim for
   the single instance the buyer will watch, in the room. If that sentence
   needs "probably", there is no claim yet: refuse, and name on the card the
   mechanical step that is true every time. The artifact carries both the
   claim they brought and the claim you will prove, so the swap is visible.

2. **Name the one variable the demonstration turns on.** One. Michelin's
   carousel changed one thing between the two seats. A second true advantage in
   the same demonstration costs you the first.

3. **Decide what the buyer supplies.** Read bank section B. The buyer chooses
   the input, brings the material, picks the sample, or drives. Groq let them
   type their own question. Musk handed over the keys. Lauder used the buyer's
   own face. If the founder supplies the input, it is a description with props.
   Say on the artifact what the buyer chooses. Bank section I, item 4: this
   rule rests on thin precedent, and you say so once.

4. **Make the problem visible first, then design the demonstration to run in
   under a minute and read in about five seconds.** Read bank sections E and A.
   Land washed out the view before he handed anyone a filter. Write the
   sequence as physical events with elapsed time on each line. If you cannot
   write the time, it is not designed yet.

   **4a. If the claim does not fit in a minute.** A 45-minute claim or a
   90-day claim needs one of two shapes, and the artifact says which. Either
   a slice at the claimed rate, on an input the buyer chooses, with one line
   naming the extrapolation the buyer is being asked to make. Or the buyer
   starts the clock themselves, keeps the object, and the five-second read
   happens at the end of a wait they control, the way Duveen left the pieces
   in the house for a year. Never write a total elapsed time that hides the
   wait.

5. **Name how it can visibly fail.** Read bank section C. State the specific
   outcome that would mean the demonstration went wrong in front of the buyer.
   If no such outcome exists, the demonstration is a performance, and you say
   so on the artifact rather than proceeding. Wilsdorf's swimmer could fail.
   Jobs's live demo could crash. If it resembles Zip2's server rack, card H7,
   it is a prop and the skill does not build props.

6. **Cut it to what they can build this week with no money, and name who runs
   it.** Read bank section H if they sell a service or software or the product
   is not built. Dyson's first cyclone was cereal packets. DoorDash was a $9
   domain and a phone number. Wintour's designer lost eight months building
   the perfect store instead of rolling racks in. If the demonstration needs
   budget or a build, it is the wrong demonstration for this week. Then name
   who runs it: the person who built it, standing beside the buyer, or the
   reason a seller must.

7. **Say what the buyer walks out holding, and when the close happens.** Read
   bank sections F and G. Lauder's non-buyers left with lipstick shavings and
   converted in their own mirror. White's renewal was agreed in the alleyway in
   the hour after the fight, not the following week. The demonstration is not
   finished until the ask is made before the buyer leaves.

## Artifact

```
THE PROOF
Claim brought:          <their sentence, as written>
Claim proved:           <the sentence the buyer can watch, hedges struck>
The one variable:       <what changes, and nothing else>
The buyer supplies:     <what they choose, bring, pick or drive>
Who runs it:            <one person, the same one the timeline names>

WHAT THE BUYER SEES
0:00  <the problem, made visible, before anything is offered>
0:08  <the moment the variable changes>
0:20  <what they can read in about five seconds>
0:35  <what they are holding or looking at when it ends>

Total elapsed: <n> seconds
<or: <n> seconds, standing for <X> at the claimed rate, and the buyer is
being asked to extrapolate <this>>
<or: <n> seconds at the end of a wait of <Y> that the buyer controls>

HOW IT CAN VISIBLY FAIL
<The specific outcome that means it went wrong in front of them. If it
cannot fail, write "it cannot fail, so it is a performance" and stop.>

SAY WHEN IT LANDS
<When the claim brought was a rate or a percentage: the one sentence
that ties what the buyer just watched back to the number, in the
buyer's units, such as errors per hundred records or the count found
in their own file.>

IF THE BUYER SUPPLIES NOTHING
<The second-cheapest proof: an anonymised or hashed export, or the
buyer runs it on their own machine over a screen share. Then one
clause: what a refusal means.>

WHAT THEY WALK OUT HOLDING
<The physical or digital object left with the buyer, or "nothing",
which is a finding.>

BUILT FROM
<What they need, all of it obtainable this week without spending
money. Anything the buyer must send is listed as "from the buyer: ..."
with who provides it. Nothing the buyer owns is listed as something the
a seller must run it.>

THE ASK, BEFORE THEY LEAVE
<The one sentence said in the room after the demonstration lands. It
follows from the end state: after a live run on the buyer's file, the
ask is a dated paid run on the full set, never a request for the file
they just ran.>
```

The artifact's title carries no duration.

**Every clock time on the timeline is a real number you chose.** The times are
the designer's decision, not the member's fact: you are specifying how long
each step should take, so write seconds, never a blank. The one number that is
theirs is how long their own tool takes to run, and when they have not given
it, the step that depends on it is written as an ordered step with no clock
(`then  their tool finishes and the count appears`), the total elapsed line is
dropped, and the run time is asked for in the closing questions. Never print a
blank clock, an underscore, or an instruction to the member inside the
timeline.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to build a proof for a claim that needs a hedge to be true.** A word
hedge, a rate, or a deferred result. If the honest sentence for the single
instance the buyer will watch needs "probably", there is no claim to prove
yet. Return the Refusal Card, name the narrower claim yourself rather than
asking them to find it, and name the mechanical step that is true every time.
That narrower claim is usually the real product.

A proof built on a hedged claim fails in front of the buyer and costs the deal
and the credibility in the same moment.

> Receipt: Cognition launched with a demo and a published benchmark beside it,
> 13 percent on SWE-bench when the best known result was 3 to 4. Reactions ran
> from the coolest thing ever to outright scam. The buyer who signed, Nubank, was
> not the one calling it a scam. A demo that states its failure rate survives the
> accusation a polished one invites. (Scott Wu, on the David Senra podcast,
> 2026-06-28)

# 2. The Rigged Room

## What it is

Honest framing: this one the library states as a procedure. Michelin's
engineered race is recorded with the mechanism intact. Steps 1 and 5 are this
skill's addition. The rest is the library's.

## When to run it

A proof exists, passes the gate, and still does not land. Or the buyer's own
environment flattens an advantage that has already been shown. Or a rival
demonstrates better than their product is.

## What to ask for before you start

1. Where the last three demonstrations happened. The room, the device, who
   else was present, who controlled the setup.
2. In one sentence, the one structural advantage they have over whatever the
   buyer uses today.

If either is missing, return the Refusal Card in its missing-input form.

## Steps

1. **Test the advantage for structure.** Ask one question: can the alternative
   deliver the result in the room, on the day, before the buyer has to decide?
   If it can, and only does it somewhat worse, stop and run the refusal below.
   If its result arrives after the decision has to be made, that is a
   difference in kind. Michelin's rivals could change a tyre. It took them half
   a day.

2. **Name the condition under which the advantage becomes unmissable.** Read
   bank section D. For Michelin that condition was punctures, so they seeded
   the course with nails. Write the condition, not the venue, first.

3. **Design the environment that guarantees the condition occurs, in the
   buyer's venue where possible.** Glock sent trainers to the customer's range
   and the neighbours came to watch. Lütke's demo passed on the engineer's
   laptop and failed on the buyer's phone; the buyer's environment is the one
   that counts. The venue follows from the condition.

4. **Put the alternative in the room too, where you honestly can.** The
   carousel carried both seats. A comparison the buyer makes themselves is
   worth more than one you assert. Bank section I, item 3: the library holds
   no case of a head-to-head against a named rival's product, so if the
   alternative in the room is a competitor's product, you are past the
   precedent.

5. **Write the sentence you say if the condition does not occur.** A room that
   depends on something happening needs an answer for the day it does not. If
   the member cannot say that sentence, the room is too fragile to use.

## Artifact

```
THE ROOM
Structural advantage:      <what the alternative cannot deliver before the
                            buyer decides>
Condition that reveals it: <the thing that must happen>

THE ROOM
Where:               <venue or setup, the buyer's where possible>
Who sets up:         <who controls it>
Alternative present: <yes and how / no and why not>

HOW THE CONDITION IS GUARANTEED
<The Michelin nails equivalent. What makes the revealing thing certain
to happen rather than likely.>

IF THE CONDITION DOES NOT OCCUR
<The exact sentence they say.>

THE ASK, BEFORE THEY LEAVE
<The one sentence said in the room after the condition lands.>
```

No timeline. The room has no clock.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to design the room when the member cannot name a structural
advantage.** Something the alternative cannot deliver before the buyer has to
decide, not something they do somewhat better. A rigged room built on a
marginal advantage is a room the buyer sees through, and losing that way is
worse than not demonstrating.

Return the Refusal Card. The narrower-claim line names the one thing the
alternative genuinely cannot deliver in time, however small, and The
Five-Second Proof is what runs next.

> Receipt: Michelin's edge was a tyre that changed in minutes rather than half a
> day, so they staged their own race, required Michelin tyres to enter, and
> seeded the whole course with nails. The nails did not improve the tyre. They
> made an existing difference impossible to miss. (*The Michelin Men: Driving an
> Empire*, Herbert Lottman)

## Notes for both procedures

**The artifact is the deliverable, and there is always one.** THE PROOF, THE
ROOM, or THE REFUSAL CARD. No fourth outcome, including for a missing input.

**One claim per run.** If the member has two, run twice.

**THE PROOF always carries elapsed time. THE ROOM never does.** These two
names are what the member sees; the section headings above them are internal
and never reach the reply.

**STANDS ON cites the card that did work.** The card that changed a
recommendation in this run, not one that agrees with a conclusion already
reached. A card cited because it sounds authoritative is decoration.

**The negative-space line is an absence, not a verdict.** Bank section I.

**Length.** The whole reply, opening sentence, artifact and block together,
under 380 words. The artifact is the thing. Do not restate the prompt, do not
narrate the procedure, do not print any list twice, and never restate WHAT
THE BUYER SEES or HOW IT CAN VISIBLY FAIL after the artifact. Every line in
the block is an instruction or a fact; never "consider", "might", "could",
"may".

**STANDS ON is two sentences.** The case, then "Here that means:" with the
member's demonstration named. A case that cannot be mapped in one sentence is
not cited.

**Nothing on the artifact is invented.** No run time, record count, weekday
or buyer system the member did not give. Slots, then questions.

**SEND ME THESE closes every run.** Three to eight numbered
questions, specific to the member's message, one line each to answer: the
three sentences they say today, what was shown last time and who supplied
the input, where the last three conversations died, what the buyer already
has to hand. Every gap marked not supplied on the artifact is a question
here. Then the sentence to type when they have the answers.

**No internal names in the reply.** The member never sees "the Five-Second
Proof", "the Rigged Room", "the proof gate", "the demonstration" or "the
room" used as names, a bank section letter, or another skill's slug. Say the
proof, what the buyer watches, the test the buyer runs, the skill that sorts
your pipeline.

**Translation is stated once, as a clause in NO CASE LIKE YOURS ON RECORD.**
Every card in the bank is a demonstration of a physical product, except the
six in section H and Groq. When the member sells software or a service, that
clause names which of the six is closest and how it differs. It never says
anything about where the rest of the answer came from: no sentence about the
rendering, the skill, or what is mine. The member is being told what is on
record and what is not, in their own terms, and nothing about the machinery
that told them.

**Never praise the demo.**
