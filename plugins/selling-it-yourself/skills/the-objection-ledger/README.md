# The Objection Ledger

Part of **Selling It Yourself**. Version 1.8.

You keep hearing the same things. Too expensive. We already have something.
Not this quarter. You have an answer for each one, and you keep losing
anyway.

Most of what buyers say is not what kills the deal. They say it and buy. This
skill counts which doubts appear only in the deals that died, so you build
proof against those and stop building against the rest.

## What you get back

One of three things, depending on what you bring.

**The count.** Bring the exact words of every objection from your last ten or
more conversations, each marked closed or died. You get every doubt grouped in
the buyer's words, with a count of how many deals closed with it raised and
how many died with it raised. A doubt that shows up in closed deals is noise.
A doubt that shows up only in dead deals is live. Politeness and stalls stay
on the sheet marked as not an objection. Then, only when a doubt is live, the
next doubts, each one either already heard once from a signer or the next
rung of the one sequence the library holds, with the proof each would need.
Then one proof to start building this week.

**The answer.** Bring one live objection in the buyer's words, its
row from the stack, your current answer, and one customer by name who bought
over it, or the word none. You get the objection restated, no longer than they
said it, and a one-sentence answer with two parts: their doubt, then a market
they already pay into that has the exact same supposed flaw. Every adjective
replaced with a number or a name, or cut. And the version that customer could
say instead of you.

**The rehearsal.** Bring one meeting this week that matters. You get the three
branches the conversation can take, each named by something a buyer who could
sign has actually said, your move on each, every point where you reached for
a slide instead of a proof, the ratio of hours rehearsed to meeting length,
and the ask as one sentence, said out loud five times. If you have not run it
out loud yet, those fields say so and the run itself is this week's task. If
you flinch saying the ask, the skill stops and tells you the problem is the
offer.

## Every reply ends with questions

Whatever it gives you back, the last thing on the page is a short numbered
list: everything it needed and did not have, asked in one go, each answerable
in a line. Answer them and re-run it, and the next version is built on facts
instead of gaps. It never asks one question at a time.

## What it refuses to do

It will not build proof against a doubt you have heard once. Unproven until
the second time.

It will not treat a doubt as live if buyers raised it and bought anyway.

It will not invent a precedent. If no market the buyer already accepts does
the thing, it says the precedent does not exist and names what proof would
have to exist instead.

It will not give you a script, a framework, or a named method. None is in
its sources.

It will not polish a pitch you cannot say with conviction.

And it will not tell you the objections are handled. There is no praise line.

## Where it comes from

Thirty-six verified cases, most of them ones nobody volunteers.

Akio Morita, told by everyone inside Sony that a player which could not record
would not sell, pointed at the millions of car stereos already sold that also
recorded nothing. Todd Graves got his loan when he put In-N-Out into the pitch
as living proof that one menu could last decades. Joseph Duveen acted out
every important sale with his secretary the day before, mapping the branches.
James Dyson watched one prospect stall him for eight months on handle redesigns
while a Japanese firm closed in three weeks, and learned to read slowness as
the answer. Barnett Helzberg made a thirty-second pitch to Warren Buffett on a
New York street, then nearly killed the deal by not sending the numbers.

Every one is traced to the book or interview it came from. The skill will not
tell you anything about these people that is not in its own files, and it does
not search the web.

It also carries a list of what those files hold no case of. Among them: no
founder is recorded repeating a buyer's objection back word for word before
answering, no founder closed a live sale by pointing at a named peer of the
buyer already using the product, and only one founder in the library ever
rehearsed a sale with someone playing the buyer. When your situation touches
one of those, the skill says so.

## Install

You need the marketplace added once. In Claude Code:

```bash
/plugin marketplace add socialspire/founder-tools
```

Then install the course:

```bash
/plugin install selling-it-yourself@socialspire
```

Start a new conversation and paste what you have:

> here are the last twelve calls, what each buyer said, and which ones closed

You do not need to name the skill. If it does not fire, say `which of these
objections is actually killing deals`.

## What to bring

For the count: ten or more sales conversations. For each, the buyer's exact
words, whether it closed or died, and whether the person speaking could sign.

For the answer: one objection in the buyer's words, your current answer, and
one market the buyer already accepts without question.

For the rehearsal: the meeting, the buyer, and the single decision you want
out of it.

The buyer's exact words are the field people skip. Your summary of what they
said is not an objection. If you cannot produce their words, that
conversation is not logged.

## If it gets something wrong

Tell us what you typed and what came back. A doubt marked live that buyers
raised and bought anyway, an invented precedent, a famous method that should
not be there, or a verdict that went soft when it should have held.
