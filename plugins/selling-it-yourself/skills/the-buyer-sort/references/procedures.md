# Procedures: The Buyer Sort

Two procedures, written as instructions to the AI running them on a member's
real material.

**Attribution rule applies here too: paraphrase only, never quotation marks,
name the book.** Cards and sources are in `bank.md`.

Run on what the member brought. Do not ask first and do not read the
situation back; the reply opens with the verdict, and every gap becomes a
question in SEND ME THESE at the end. See Step 1 of `SKILL.md`. Never stop in
prose to ask.

---

## Routing

| What the member brought | Run | Bank sections to read |
|---|---|---|
| Open deals that have stalled, a pipeline they cannot read, a buyer who will not close | The sort | A, B, C, D, G, H, plus E when any deal is blocked by a gatekeeper rather than by its own revenue, or the member sells a service |
| A cleared or empty pipeline, a sort already run, and no idea who to approach | The target list | A, B, C, F, G, H |

**If they have never sorted, run the sort first even if they asked for the
list.** Say why in one line and run it. Never run both in one pass.

**Read only the sections routed.** The bank is long on purpose and the member
is waiting.

---

# 0. The Refusal Card

**Read this before either procedure.** It is what a refusal returns, and it is
also what a missing input returns. A stop with no card is the fourth outcome
this skill does not have.

```
CANNOT SORT THIS YET

WHAT YOU BROUGHT
<Their pipeline or their target list, in one line, with the count.>

WHY IT WILL NOT SORT
<The specific defect. Two lines at most. For a missing input this reads
"cannot run yet: <the input> is missing".>

WHAT I WOULD SORT INSTEAD
<The narrower set that can be sorted today, if any. If none survives,
write "none survives" and say what has to happen first.>

SEND ME THESE
<Numbered. Every fact this run needed and did not get, as questions they
can answer in one line each, specific to what they wrote. Three to eight,
all at once, never one at a time.>

WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT
<One line, plain words: what runs next once they answer, or the thing
that has to happen first, such as a first paying customer. Ends with:
Send these back and I will redo the sheet.>
```

Do not apologise and do not soften. No praise line.

**The card is total or it is not a card.** It returns only when no deal or no
name survives. When some deals are missing what the buyer sells, or some
target names carry no fact, the artifact is still the sheet or the list, with
the unsortable rows named under the header. See each procedure's refusal.

**After the card, the output block shrinks to two lines:** STANDS ON and NO CASE
LIKE YOURS ON RECORD. The card already carries the next move and the
re-run sentence. Never name a likely column for a deal the refusal set aside.
That is a placement on one contact, and step 1 forbids it.

# 1. The sort

## What it is

Honest framing: the library does not present this as a named or numbered
method. It presents cases about who can and cannot buy, from founders who
never met, and one line, Clark's, that states the principle outright. Steps 2,
3, 4 and 5 are the library's own distinctions. Steps 1, 6, 7, 8 and 9 are this
skill's rendering of them into a runnable sequence.

## When to run it

The member has open deals that are not moving, or cannot say which deals are
worth their hours.

## What to ask for before you start

Every open deal. For each one:

1. The company or person.
2. **What they sell, in a few words.** The load-bearing input. Without it you
   cannot see whose revenue your product removes.
3. How many times they have been contacted.
4. The exact words of their most recent reply.
5. Whether they have ever paid anything.
6. Hours spent on the deal this quarter, or if unknown, the number of meetings.
7. Any decision date the buyer has given, or none.
8. For a services, agency or software buyer: whether they bill their own
   clients by time or by outcome. The Gould test cannot run on them without it.

## Steps

1. **Write the mechanism assumption, then discard every deal with only one
   contact.** The assumption is one line on the sheet header: how the product
   changes the buyer's money. When the message does not settle it, no deal is
   filed as unable to buy, the verdict is conditional, and it is question 1.
   One contact is evidence of one contact, not of a no; those deals go under
   UNSORTED.

2. **Place the impossible column first, and weigh both sides before you do.**
   For each remaining deal, ask: does a revenue line of theirs have to shrink
   for yours to grow, under the assumption written? If yes, it goes here
   regardless of how warm it feels. Arithmetic, not judgment. Read bank
   section A. Walton at Ben Franklin is the numeric analogue: a cut from 25
   percent to roughly 12 produced an explosion, not a deal.

   **A buyer who both sells the thing and consumes it is not automatically
   blocked.** A commercial print shop sells printing and is also one of the
   heaviest buyers of printing inputs there is. If the product cuts what a
   customer pays a vendor, the shop's revenue shrinks and it is blocked. If it
   cuts the cost of producing the thing, the shop's own cost of goods falls
   and it is the best buyer in the pipeline, not the worst. The same holds for
   an agency that resells a service it also buys, or a manufacturer that
   supplies a part it also uses.

   So for any buyer on both sides of the trade, write one line: what they
   sell that shrinks, and what they buy that gets cheaper. A deal goes to the
   blocked column only when the shrinking side is the larger one and the
   message settles which side the product touches. When it does not settle
   it, the deal goes to UNSORTED with that question as its move, and the
   verdict sentence says the ruling turns on it. Filing the heaviest consumer
   of a thing as unable to buy a tool that makes the thing cheaper is the
   most expensive mistake this procedure can make.

3. **Run the Gould test on every deal in the impossible column.** Read bank
   section B. Western Union refused the phonograph and bought the quadruplex,
   because the quadruplex multiplied messages per existing line. Ask: is there
   a version of this product that raises what the buyer produces from what it
   already owns, rather than cutting what its customers spend?

   A version counts only if all three hold. It raises the output of something
   the buyer already owns without asking the buyer to change how it prices.
   The buyer has not already heard it. And for a services, agency or software
   buyer, you know whether they bill time or outcome, because a throughput
   pitch to a time-biller is a pitch to cut their own hours. If the buyer
   voiced the throughput line themselves and did not sign, write "already
   tried by the buyer, none exists". If the billing model is unknown, that is
   the missing input on that row, not a pass.

   Do not invent a throughput pitch to rescue a deal. The library holds one
   clean precedent for this and no services or software case of it, and the
   sheet says so.

   Two further screens from section B: has this buyer ever bought a threat
   before (Murphy)? Is it visibly losing to a peer that already adopted what
   you sell (Bugatti)? Either one moves it toward the explaining column.

4. **Place the excited column.** Read bank section C. A deal belongs here only
   if the buyer described the use themselves, in their own words, or paid
   before delivery (Cucinelli), or ordered past the member's own plan
   (Mellon). Quote the member's record of what they said. Enthusiasm the
   member inferred does not count.

5. **Everything left is the explaining column.** Read bank section D. Sort
   them by what they argue about (Del Vecchio): precision means nearly sold,
   price means not, not yet argued means the signer has not been met. Then
   apply Dyson's clock: a deal that has missed a decision deadline goes to the
   bottom of this column and its next move is the closing line. A deal with no
   deadline gets one as its next move. A missed deadline never moves a deal
   into the impossible column. That column is structural only.

6. **Name who signs only where the member's words say.** Three shapes to
   catch when the message names the role: a franchise location or branch that
   cannot buy without a parent (Walton pitched Ben Franklin's executives, not
   a store manager, card A3); a champion with enthusiasm and no budget; a yes
   that rests on one person's tenure (the Nobels' Tsar, card F2). A deal whose
   named contact cannot sign goes in WRONG ROOM, whatever column it would
   otherwise sit in. When the message does not name the contact's role, the
   row carries no signer and the question is asked once at the end; the
   header line "Signer confirmed: n of N" carries the count.

7. **Write a next move on every row, with a day.** Impossible rows get the
   complete closing message. Deciding rows get the single task that advances
   them: the comparison procurement asked for, built on stated assumptions
   and sent with the request to correct them; the invoice request; the call
   to the signer. Ready rows get the start date. Wrong-room rows get the one
   step to reach the signer. Unsorted rows get the ask that decides them. A
   row with a verdict and no dated move is a defect.

8. **Count the meetings.** Use the hours the member gave. If they gave
   meetings, use one hour each and say so. Count only what the message gives;
   a deal with no count is named under the table, never written as unknown.
   The arrow goes on the larger of the last two rows. That number is the
   finding.

9. **Order DO THIS WEEK by the verdict.** The deal named first in the verdict
   sentence gets the first dated action; every other deal named in the
   verdict follows in order; drops come after the spend actions, never
   instead of them. If the impossible column is empty, the finding is the
   wrong-room count, and the first action is reaching the one signer with the
   most meetings behind them.

## Artifact

```
WHO CAN SAY YES
Reading your tool as: <the mechanism assumption, one line>
Open deals: <n>    Signer confirmed: <n> of <N>    Meetings counted: <n>

READY TO START — they described the use themselves
  <company, their own label for it>  |  their words: <what they said>
             |  next move: do <day>: <the start date, and to whom>

STILL DECIDING — logical buyers who should want it
  <company>  |  meetings: <n>  |  last words: <what they said>
             |  next move: send <day>: <complete message> / do <day>: <action>

WRONG ROOM — the person you know cannot sign, from your own words
  <company>  |  talking to: <role, as they wrote it>
             |  next move: do <day>: <the one step to reach the signer>

CANNOT BUY — their own revenue shrinks if they say yes
  <company>  |  what of theirs shrinks: <the line>
             |  a version they could buy: <it, or none, or cannot say until Q<n>>
             |  next move: send <day>: <complete closing message>

UNSORTED — cannot be placed until you answer a question below
  <company>  |  decides it: Q<n>  |  next move: do <day>: <the ask on the call>

MEETINGS SO FAR  (count only what you gave; meetings stay meetings)
  Ready to start    <n>
  Still deciding    <n>
  Cannot buy        <n>   <- the arrow goes on the larger of the last two
  <a deal with no count given is named here, not written as unknown>
```

The version-they-could-buy result lives on the row, once. A field the
message does not supply is left off the row: no "unknown", no "find out",
no inferred role. Sections and rows whose count is zero are omitted. A deal
whose test cannot run sits under UNSORTED with the question that decides it,
never under CANNOT BUY, and never with "stopping outreach" as its move.

**Every next move carries a day and is either "send <day>:" followed by the
complete message or "do <day>:" followed by the action.** A message is
written as the member, two to four sentences, readable aloud, ending with one
yes-or-no question. The drop message: thank them for the meetings, say the
file is closing, name the one thing that would reopen it. The start message:
the date the member can start, and the question of who signs on their side.

**Every cell is one line, under fifteen words**, except a complete message,
which is the one multi-line cell. A cell holds a fact or a move, never the
reasoning behind it. If the reasoning matters, it belongs in WHAT IS WRONG
WITH THIS, once.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to sort a pipeline in which no deal has been contacted more than
once.** Return the Refusal Card, name the follow-up as the next action, and
give the count awaiting a second contact.

**Refuses when nobody has ever paid for the product.** Free pilots are not a
customer. This skill sorts buyer behaviour and there is none yet. Return the
Refusal Card: WHAT I WOULD SORT INSTEAD reads "none survives", WHAT I NEED
FROM YOU asks who was the first person to pay anything and what they said,
and WHEN YOU ANSWER THAT names a first paying customer as the thing that has
to happen, not a procedure.

**Partial gaps are not a refusal.** When some deals are missing what the buyer
sells, sort the rest and list the missing ones under the header line. The
card returns only when no deal survives.

> Receipt: Western Union saw no use at all in Edison's phonograph. The same
> company bought Jay Gould's quadruplex, which multiplied the messages a single
> existing line could carry. One product cut what their customers spent. The
> other raised what their own wires could do. (*American Rascal*, Greg Steinmetz)

# 2. The target list

## What it is

Honest framing: this one the library states plainly, and it is card F6 in the
bank. Ogilvy wrote down five accounts the day after opening and all five
eventually became clients. The number, the timing and the value-delivery
mechanism are the library's. The qualifying filters in steps 2 and 3 are drawn
from separate cards. Step 5 is this skill's addition.

## When to run it

The pipeline has been sorted and cleared, or there is no pipeline, and the
member does not know who to approach.

## What to ask for before you start

Five accounts they most want and cannot plausibly win today. For each: one
thing they actually know about that business, a hire, a launch, a public
number, or a complaint they have seen it make. And, if they know it, who does
this job for that business today. If they do not know, the row reads
"unknown, find out" and the value-delivery item is how they find out. That is
not a refusal.

## Steps

1. **Take the five names.** If they offer more than five, cut to five.

2. **Test each for an unfilled need.** Is anyone already doing this job for
   them adequately? If yes, strike the name. Ogilvy solicited advertisers with
   no agency at all rather than dislodging incumbents. Ask for a replacement.

3. **Test each against the impossible column and the Gould test.** Read bank
   sections A and B. Does a revenue line of theirs shrink if they buy? If yes
   and no throughput version exists, strike it.

4. **Prefer the buyer who sets taste, and the buyer who pays fastest.** Read
   bank sections C and G. Glock won the institutional buyer whose choices the
   civilian market copied. Ovitz started in television because talent got paid
   every Thursday. Name who signs at each account.

5. **Write the value-delivery item for each, and a send date.** Not a pitch. A
   genuinely useful document, tailored, worth reading if they never bought
   anything. Ogilvy mailed substantive progress reports to 600 people and was
   hired by a Seagram founder who quoted a 16-page speech back at him. A
   different date for each, inside four weeks.

## Artifact

```
THE TARGET LIST

1  <company>
   What I know:      <the hire, launch, number or complaint>
   Unfilled need:    <who does this for them now, or "unknown, find out">
   Can they say yes: <yes / no, and the revenue line if no>
   Signs:            <who>
   Sets taste or pays fast: <which, if either>
   I will send:      <the useful document, named>
   Send by:          <date>

2  <company>
   ...

STRUCK FROM THE LIST
  <name>  |  <already well served / cannot say yes / nothing known>
```

On a target-list run, the output block replaces WHO CANNOT SAY YES with one
line, STRUCK, WHY: the count struck and the reason for each.

## Refusal condition

**Refuses to write the list when all five accounts are already well served by
an existing provider.** Return the Refusal Card. Hand the five back and ask
for five where nobody is currently doing the job.

**A name with nothing known is struck, not refused.** It goes under STRUCK
FROM THE LIST as "nothing known". The card returns only when no name survives.

> Receipt: The day after opening his agency, Ogilvy wrote down the five accounts
> he most wanted, at a point where he had no plausible claim on any of them. All
> five eventually became clients. He called it an act of mad presumption himself.
> (*Confessions of an Advertising Man*, David Ogilvy)

## Notes for both procedures

**The artifact is the deliverable, and there is always one.** THE BUYER SORT
SHEET, THE TARGET LIST, or THE REFUSAL CARD. No fourth outcome on a run. An
argument turn re-issues the contested row; see SKILL.md.

**The Gould test rests on one precedent and the sheet says so.** Do not
manufacture a throughput pitch to rescue a deal the member likes.

**Impossible is structural, never difficult.** Slow, cheap, disorganised and
rude are hard buyers, not impossible ones. A missed deadline never makes a
buyer impossible.

**STANDS ON cites the card that moved a deal between columns in this run.** If
the Gould test moved one, it is Gould. Only when two cards did equal work,
take the one a strong model would not volunteer. Never a card that merely
agrees with a conclusion already reached.

**The negative-space line names a numbered item in bank section H.** If none
fits exactly, name the nearest one and say it is the nearest. Never assert an
absence H does not list. Stated as an absence, never as a verdict.

**Length.** The whole reply, opening sentence, sheet and block together, is
under 250 words plus 40 per deal. The cap is met by the cell rule above and
by omitting empty sections, not by cutting rows. Declining an out-of-source
request costs one sentence, never a paragraph. Do not restate the prompt, do
not narrate the procedure, do not print any list twice. WHAT IS WRONG WITH
THIS is two sentences or omitted. Every line in the block is an instruction
or a fact; never "consider", "might", "could", "may".

**STANDS ON is two sentences.** The case, then "Here that means:" with the
member's deal named. A case that cannot be mapped to one of their deals in
one sentence is not cited.

**Nothing on the sheet is inferred.** A role, a signer, a date or a count the
message did not give is left off and asked once at the end. The mechanism
assumption is written on the header and, when unsettled, is question 1.

**SEND ME THESE closes every run.** Three to eight
numbered questions, specific to the member's deals by name, one line each to
answer: who signs, what they bill, their exact words, decision dates,
whether anyone has paid. Every gap marked unknown on the sheet is a question
here. Then the sentence to type when they have the answers.

**No internal names in the reply.** The member never sees a procedure name, a
test named after a founder, a bank section letter, or another skill's slug.
Say the sheet, the target list, the buyers who cannot buy, the version they
could buy, the skill that builds the demonstration.

**Never praise the pipeline.** Report the hours on the wrong columns and stop.
