# The Price Hold

Part of **Selling It Yourself**. Version 1.8.

The buyer says the number is too high. You have about four seconds. Whatever
you decide in those four seconds is what you will charge, and it will be less
than you meant to.

This skill decides beforehand. It needs the real cost to deliver this deal
and the number you decided you would not go under, permits exactly one
counter at that number, and swaps terms instead of margin when the objection
turns out not to be about money at all. It never writes your walk-away for
you.

## What you get back

**The numbers on this deal.** Bring the asking price, what this one delivery costs
you in materials, hours at a real rate and third-party costs, the buyer's
exact words, and the number you decided you would not go under. You get the
margin on this deal, whether your walk-away was written before or after you
read their number, and a ruling: accept outright if their number clears the
walk-away, one counter at the walk-away if it does not, and the walk if the
counter is refused. If they have not named a number yet, the card stops there
and hands you the question to ask. It also counts consecutive walks on the
same objection, because three in a row is a finding about the offer, not the
buyers.

**The terms on this deal.** Bring a buyer whose objection is timing or risk rather
than the number. You get the objection classified into one of five, exactly
one swap, its cost to you if the buyer never converts, and the price after the
swap, which has to equal the asking price or it was a discount with a
different name.

**When it cannot rule.** If you cannot say what this deal costs to deliver, you
get the card instead of a ruling, and the questions to answer before it can
run.

## The five classes

Only one is about price.

- No money. The number is the block at any date. That is a price
  conversation.
- Cannot pay now. They want it and the money arrives later. Hold the number,
  sell them time.
- Does not believe the claim. Not a price problem. The skill that builds the
  demonstration, not this one.
- No urgency. Hold the number, move the clock.
- Too much risk. Hold the number, carry the risk.

## Every reply ends with questions

Whatever it gives you back, the last thing on the page is a short numbered
list: everything it needed and did not have, asked in one go, each answerable
in a line. Answer them and re-run it, and the next version is built on facts
instead of gaps. It never asks one question at a time.

## What it refuses to do

It will not rule on a price without the cost to deliver this specific deal. A
company-wide margin is not a cost.

It will not produce a second counter. One, at your walk-away, then walk. If
the buyer comes back with a number of their own, that number is theirs to
accept or walk from, never a reason for a second number from you.

It will not counter before the buyer has named a number, asked or
volunteered.

It will not write your walk-away for you. Without it you get the card and
one question, plus everything else it needs, all at once.

It will not swap terms for a buyer who does not believe the product works.
Free inventory to a doubter is a delay.

It will not approve more than one concession per deal.

It will not set your price. It holds one that exists.

And it will not tell you the price is right. There is no praise line.

## Where it comes from

Thirty-four verified cases, most of them ones nobody volunteers.

Tom Murphy asked every seller what they thought the property was worth,
accepted a fair number outright, gave one best price if it was high, and
walked if that was refused. He never won an auction. Joseph Duveen, told a
million and a half was too high, left the pieces in the buyer's house for a
year on option at the full price, and was paid the day before it expired.
Estée Lauder, told the counters were locked up by exclusives, pitched a bath
oil on consignment so the store risked nothing. Andrew Carnegie was the only
steelmaker who knew his cost per ton, so every price he named was a known
margin while his rivals' were guesses. Larry Ellison let quarter-end
discounting run for years and then spent years undoing what it had taught
buyers about when to push.

Every one is traced to the book or interview it came from. The skill will not
tell you anything about these people that is not in its own files, and it does
not search the web.

It also carries a list of what those files hold no case of. The first item is
the honest one: no founder in the library is recorded answering a price
objection with a single counter and holding. The rule is Murphy's buying loop
mirrored, and the skill says so. Nobody in the library won by lowering a price
because a prospect pushed back, either. Every low-price winner set the price
by design before any buyer conversation. When your situation touches one of
those, the skill says so.

## Install

You need the marketplace added once. In Claude Code:

```bash
/plugin marketplace add socialspire/founder-tools
```

Then install the course:

```bash
/plugin install selling-it-yourself@socialspire
```

Start a new conversation and paste what you have:

> they said 4,800 is more than they budgeted, here's what the job costs me,
> what do I say back

You do not need to name the skill. If it does not fire, say `hold my price on
this deal`.

## What to bring

The asking price. What this one delivery costs you: materials, hours at the
rate you would pay someone else, third-party costs. The buyer's exact words
about the price. Whether they have named a number, asked or unasked. And the
number you decided you would not go under, ideally before their email came
in.

The cost and the walk-away are the fields people skip. A walk-away invented
in the room is the buyer's number with your name on it, and the skill will
not invent one for you either.

## If it gets something wrong

Tell us what you typed and what came back. A second counter that got through,
a margin percentage accepted as a cost, a swap that was really a discount, or
a ruling that went soft when it should have held.
