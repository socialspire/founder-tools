# SELL OR ELSE

Paste your copy. Get it graded and rewritten.

---

## What this actually does for you

You wrote a headline. You are not sure if it is any good. Nobody around you will tell you the truth, and the people who would tell you the truth are your customers, who just leave.

This grades the copy instead. Forty-eight checks across ten areas. Every failing line comes back quoted, with the rule it broke and the replacement line already written for you.

You can paste the new headline into your page before you close the session. That is the point. It does not hand you advice about how to write better copy. It hands you the copy.

It is not kind. It will tell you the headline is a label, the promise is missing, and the button says nothing. But it never leaves you with a problem and no fix. Every criticism comes with the fixed line underneath it.

Each rule also comes with one line of evidence. Something a real founder actually did, with the book or the interview named. Not so you go read them. So you can check that the rule is real before you take the advice.

---

## Install it

Nothing to do here. This tool installed with the rest of them in one step.
The classroom lesson **Install the tools** covers it once, in about three minutes.

## Use it

Paste three things. The copy, what it is meant to do, and who is meant to read it.

```
Review my copy.

This is the hero section of my landing page. It is meant to get someone
to book a 20-minute demo.

Reader: bookkeepers working on their own, with 5 to 15 small business
clients, doing everything in spreadsheets.

The all-in-one platform for modern bookkeeping.
We help small businesses streamline their finances with innovative,
easy-to-use tools. Our seamless solution is trusted by businesses everywhere.
[Get Started]
```

If you leave out who it is for, it will ask once. It cannot grade copy without a reader, and it will not pretend it can.

**If the copy is already running and making you money, say so and give the number.** It will tell you not to replace it. Working copy gets challenged, not overwritten.

Two to four minutes for a headline or a subject line. Five to ten for a full page.

---

## What comes back

Three parts, always in this order.

**1. The report card.** The single worst thing about the copy, named in one sentence, before anything else. Then a score out of 100 with ten section scores under it.

**2. The line-by-line.** Your line quoted. The rule it broke. The evidence for that rule. The replacement line, finished and ready to paste.

**3. The one change.** If you do nothing else, this is the edit.

## A real example

Here is what the copy above gets back. Shortened. The real report goes up to eight lines.

### THE REPORT CARD

```
WORST OFFENSE: Every sentence is addressed to small businesses. Your
reader is the bookkeeper. The copy talks straight past the person who
is supposed to buy it. (R29)

SCORE: 10/100. This is not copy yet. It is a description of a category.

A. The Promise                0/14   Nothing in 24 words says what the reader gets
B. The Big Idea               2/10   Three different phrasings of the same empty claim
C. Facts vs Adjectives        0/10   Zero checkable facts in the whole section
D. Specificity and Numbers    0/6    No number anywhere
E. Headline / Opening Line    4/20   Six words, and none of them is a promise
F. Clarity and Plain Language 2/8    Readable. Nothing to repeat back.
G. Reader's Self-Interest     0/10   Written for the wrong person
H. Proof and Credibility      0/10   Trusted by businesses everywhere is not proof
I. The Close and the Ask      1/6    One button, no reason to press it
J. Length and Boredom         1/6    Five claims, none of them interesting

Reader graded against: solo bookkeepers, 5 to 15 clients, working in spreadsheets
Job graded against: book a 20-minute demo
```

### THE LINE-BY-LINE

```
YOUR LINE: "The all-in-one platform for modern bookkeeping."
BREAKS:    R2 and R19. A label, not a promise.
WHY:       Any competitor could paste their name over this and it would
           still be true. All-in-one is what you write when you have not
           decided what the one thing is.
RECEIPT:   Five times as many people read the headline as read the body,
           so writing it spends eighty cents of the advertising dollar.
           A headline change can swing sales tenfold. Ogilvy wrote no
           fewer than sixteen headlines per ad.
           (Confessions of an Advertising Man, David Ogilvy)
REWRITE:   Close fifteen clients' books in [the number of hours it
           actually takes], not across every weekend of the month.

           Fill the blank with a real number, from your own use or from
           one customer. If you do not have that number, you cannot run
           this headline yet, and the fix is not wording.
```

```
YOUR LINE: "We help small businesses streamline their finances with
           innovative, easy-to-use tools."
BREAKS:    R4 and R29. Wrong reader, and you are the subject of your
           own sentence.
WHY:       Your reader is the bookkeeper. This sentence is talking to
           their client. We help is a hedge. Innovative and easy-to-use
           carry no fact a person could check.
RECEIPT:   Selling door to door, Ogilvy found that the more information
           he gave, the more he sold. His Rolls-Royce advertising
           carried facts and no gracious-living puffery.
           (Confessions of an Advertising Man, David Ogilvy)
REWRITE:   You keep the spreadsheets you already use. Upload one and
           get back a reconciled ledger, with every entry that does not
           match the bank already flagged.

           Ship only the parts of that sentence that are literally true
           of your product today.
```

```
YOUR LINE: "Our seamless solution is trusted by businesses everywhere."
BREAKS:    R35 and R38. A superlative with nothing behind it.
WHY:       Everywhere is not a place. If you have three customers, three
           is a stronger word than everywhere, because three is checkable.
RECEIPT:   In July 1914 Rolex won the first Class A chronometer
           certificate ever awarded to a wristwatch. The certificate made
           every subsequent ad more persuasive because it converted a
           claim into a verified fact.
           (Rolex Jubilee Vade Mecum, Vol. 1, Hans Wilsdorf, 1946)
REWRITE:   Cut the line. If you have customers, replace it with: [N]
           bookkeepers close their month on this. If you have none, say
           nothing. Do not swap one unverifiable claim for another.
```

```
YOUR LINE: "Get Started"
BREAKS:    R40. A bare instruction with no reason.
RECEIPT:   Adding a reason dramatically increased the likelihood of
           people saying yes.
           (Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion)
REWRITE:   Book the 20-minute demo, because the fastest way to know if
           this saves you a weekend is to watch it run on one of your
           own client files.
```

### THE ONE CHANGE

```
Change: "The all-in-one platform for modern bookkeeping."
To:     "Close fifteen clients' books in [the number of hours it
         actually takes], not across every weekend of the month."
Why:    Five times as many people will read that line than anything
        else on the page, and right now it names nothing the reader wants.
```

### BEFORE YOU REPLACE IT

You are advertising to a moving parade, not a standing army. The audience refreshes, so the ad that worked last year works on this year's arrivals.
Retire an asset when the numbers fall, not when you get bored of it. Set a performance threshold, not a calendar date. Both points are paraphrased from *Confessions of an Advertising Man*, David Ogilvy.

---

## What it will not do

**It will not invent facts about your business.** No customer counts, no prices, no results. If a rewrite needs a number only you have, it leaves a labelled blank and tells you exactly what to go find out.

**It will not predict your results.** The evidence attached to each rule is history. It is not a forecast about your page. Any tool that tells you a rewrite will convert 30% better is guessing, and so would this one.

**It will not make a false claim sound better.** If your copy is weak because the claim is not true yet, it says so and stops. Better wording on an unearned claim is a worse problem, not a fix.

**It will not tell you to replace copy that is working.** Give it your numbers and it will treat its rewrite as a challenger to test against what you already have.

## Ask it for more

Once you have the report:

- `show me the other headlines` and it will. It wrote sixteen before it picked three.
- `grade the rewrite` runs the new version back through the same 48 rules.
- `do the email version` keeps the promise and changes the format.
- `why did section E score so low` shows the rule-by-rule working.
