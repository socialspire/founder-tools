# The Chairman's Deck

Not a seat. This is the material on how to make a call, used only in Step 5 when
you write the verdict. Nobody rules from this deck.

**Attribution: mixed.** Each item is marked. Paraphrase-only items get no
quotation marks, ever. Quotable items carry the show and the date.

Four figures appear here who are not council seats: Paul Graham, Peter Thiel,
Napoleon, and Phil Knight. There is one principle documented for each and nothing
more. Do not give any of them a voice, a persona, or a ruling. Use the line and
stop.

---

## 1. Is this a one-way door or a two-way door

**Distinguish two-way doors from one-way doors, and never use one decision
process for both.** Most decisions are reversible. Aging companies wrongly treat
them as permanent.
*Bezos, Invent and Wander. Paraphrase only.*

**Correct course often enough and you can be decisive moment to moment while
staying tentative about direction.** The metaphor is a running back moving
downfield, certain of the goal and flexible about the route, sometimes including
backwards.
*Paul Graham's essays. Paraphrase only.*

**A startup messed up at its foundation cannot be fixed.** Early mistakes, wrong
partners, wrong early hires, usually require something like bankruptcy before
anyone attempts a correction. Slow down on foundational decisions specifically,
and move fast only on reversible ones.
*Peter Thiel, Zero to One. Paraphrase only.*

**Use it like this:** name which door this is before you rule. Reversible gets
speed and a short feedback loop. Irreversible gets the analysis. Partners and
early hires are the foundational category.

## 2. How much information is enough

**Decide at roughly 70% of the information you wish you had.** Waiting for 90% is
too slow. If you are good at recognizing and reversing bad calls, being wrong is
cheaper than you think. Being slow is expensive for certain.
*Bezos, Invent and Wander and Working Backwards. Paraphrase only.*

**Take the input, then go inside and make the call.** Gather as much data and
opinion as possible, then set it aside and decide. That decision is the job.
*Brad Jacobs, on the David Senra podcast, 2025-10-26. Quotable.*

**Decide fast and never look back.** Five thousand tables, a thirty-year lease,
real estate, hires. She swung and moved on. No shades of gray, no second-guessing.
*Rose Blumkin, The Women of Berkshire Hathaway by Karen Linder. Paraphrase only.*

**Live conflict, do not average it.** Bezos decides at 70% and books a checkpoint.
Blumkin commits with no review clause. If the decision is a two-way door, the
checkpoint is cheap and Bezos wins. If revisiting it would cost more than being
wrong, Blumkin wins.

## 3. Invert before you rule

**Invert. Solve for what to avoid before solving for what to achieve.** His maxim,
taken from the mathematician Jacobi, was to always invert. Hard problems yield
only when addressed backwards.
*Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack. Paraphrase only, including the maxim.*

**Find what causes failure, then engineer its removal.** Munger studies how
businesses decline before studying how they grow. Buffett describes the method as
starting from failure and removing it.
→ List the ten ways this could die, rank by likelihood, eliminate the top one.
*Buffett and Munger, in All I Want to Know Is Where I'm Going to Die So I'll
Never Go There by Peter Bevelin. Paraphrase only.*

**Be paranoid while planning and relentless once you have started.** He
deliberately exaggerated every danger and contingency beforehand, sat in genuine
agitation over it, then forgot all of it the moment he committed and thought only
about what produced the result.
*Napoleon, The Mind of Napoleon, edited and translated by J. Christopher Herold.
Paraphrase only.*

**Rehearse the downside out loud.** He and Hansson practice negative
visualization, for example AI wiping out SaaS in three years. Their conclusion
was that 27 years would have been a great run, not a tragedy.
*Jason Fried, on the David Senra podcast, 2026-02-15. Quotable.*

## 4. Kill criteria, and the case for quitting

**Never stay down after a reverse.** Adversity arrives even for the lucky and
wise. Quitting after the first few severe blows is the failure mode.
*Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack. Paraphrase only.*

**Assume the work is infinitely harder than you estimate, then commit anyway.**
Imagine how hard starting will be, multiply by infinity, and if you are still in,
you will succeed. Most good ideas die because someone stopped, not because
someone lost.
*Todd Graves, on the David Senra podcast, 2025-11-09. Quotable.*

**Knowing when to give up can be genius.** Knight calls those who tell
entrepreneurs to never give up charlatans, and separates giving up from stopping.
*Phil Knight, Shoe Dog. Paraphrase only. The line about giving up appears in the
source inside quotation marks and must not be reproduced as a quote.*

**If it works, run it until it stops working.** Set a performance threshold, not a
calendar date, as the kill criterion.
*Ogilvy, Confessions of an Advertising Man. Paraphrase only.*

**Use it like this:** a council that only ever says persist is theatre. Knight is
the counterweight to Graves and Munger. When the member's real question is
whether to stop, both sides get stated.

## 5. How big should the bet be

**Take risks without putting the company at risk.** If you are forced to bet the
farm, he considers it already lost.
*Jason Fried, on the David Senra podcast, 2026-02-15. Quotable.*

**Your failures must scale with your company.** If the size of your failed
experiments is not growing, you are not inventing at a scale that moves the
needle.
*Bezos, Invent and Wander. Paraphrase only.*

**Small units make you anti-fragile.** A day, a decision, a six-week project,
small enough to throw away when wrong.
*Jason Fried, on the David Senra podcast, 2026-02-15. Quotable.*

## 6. Judgment versus proof

**Use judgment where math cannot reach.** Elasticity data predicts this week and
this quarter. Nothing numerically estimates what a decade of price cuts does.
*Bezos, Invent and Wander. Paraphrase only.*

**Expect your best decisions to be controversial.** Math-based decisions command
wide agreement. Judgment-based decisions get debated. That is the nature of the
category, not a defect.
*Bezos, Invent and Wander. Paraphrase only.*

This is the licence for the dissent block. Disagreement inside the council is the
expected signature of a real judgment call, not evidence the council failed.
Never suppress it for a cleaner page.

**Distrust spreadsheets as decision-makers.** He has never seen a spreadsheet that
made him do anything, and is wary of anything that claims extra authority because
it carries numbers.
*Jason Fried, on the David Senra podcast, 2026-02-15. Quotable.*

## 7. Writing the "flips if" line

This is the most valuable line in the output. It names the specific condition
under which the strongest dissent becomes right.

**Own your mistakes by naming the mental model that produced them.** He admitted
the land-rush framing was a useful decision aid that had expired, and replaced it
with the electric industry, a thin horizontal layer across many industries.
→ Write down the metaphor driving the strategy and ask whether it has quietly
stopped being true.
*Bezos, Invent and Wander. Paraphrase only.*

**Model being wrong out loud.** He role-models flexible rather than rigid
thinking, and treats being disproven as learning rather than losing.
*Brad Jacobs, on the David Senra podcast, 2025-10-26. Quotable.*

**Learn to ignore the example of others when they are wrong.** Peer-company
behavior gets mindlessly imitated, and high-IQ people do it too.
→ Before adopting an industry practice, write down the reason it is right
independent of who else does it.
*Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack. Paraphrase only.*

**Test for the line:** could the member check this condition inside a week? If
not, rewrite it until they can.

## 8. Is this decision even worth the seat time

**Kill anything that does not move your two core numbers.** His shorthand is
"WOT-WOM," waste of time, waste of money.
*Brad Jacobs, on the David Senra podcast, 2025-10-26. Quotable.*

**Reduce complex situations to their basic, unemotional fundamentals.** Genius has
the fewest moving parts.
→ Restate the business model in one sentence with no adjectives, and delete any
part that is not load-bearing.
*Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack. Paraphrase only.*

If the decision does not move anything, say so and hand back the ten minutes.

## 9. The conflict map

Use this in Step 2 to guarantee a real dissenter, and in Step 4 to find the
argument that is actually there.

| The question underneath | Seat A | Seat B |
|---|---|---|
| Should the business have a growth target at all | Fried: enough is a finish line, work in days, skip targets | Jacobs: go all in, five-year levers, two metrics |
| Sell equity or take a partner | Graves: hold your equity, buy the franchisees back | Jacobs: locked-up senior equity, 500-plus acquisitions, outside scrutiny is a benefit |
| Compete where you have no skill yet | Munger: play only games where you have an edge | Bezos: work backwards from the customer and build the muscle |
| How big should the bet be | Fried: cap every bet at survivable | Bezos: failures must scale so failing genuinely hurts |
| Delegate or stay in it | Munger: give full authority and stop reviewing | Graves: supplement an 85 until they hit 95 |
| Where the marketing money goes | Ogilvy: buy and perfect the advertising | Lauder: no ad department, spend it on free product |
| Persuade or state plainly | Lauder: sell hard, demonstrate, manufacture demand | Fried: refuse to convince anyone |
| Price up or price down | Graves: never a penny of margin out of the product | Blumkin: permanent 10% markup, show the supplier's bill |
| One person or the group decides | Ogilvy: no statues of committees | Jacobs: group-sourced agenda, room of twenty-five |
| Commit and never look back, or commit and check | Blumkin: no review clause | Bezos: decide at 70% and book the checkpoint |
| Persist or quit | Graves and Munger: remove the exit, never stay down after a reverse | Knight: knowing when to give up can be genius |

## 10. Where this deck ends

No material on legal structure, entity type, contracts, tax, incorporation, or
how to raise a round. No material on modern acquisition channels. When a decision
turns on any of those, say the council does not cover it rather than reasoning
past the edge.
