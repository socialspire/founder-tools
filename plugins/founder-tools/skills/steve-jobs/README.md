# Steve Jobs — a skill you install

This runs Steve Jobs's documented decision procedure on your actual work.

You paste in a landing page, an offer list, a pitch, a price, or the plan you
have been rewriting for three weeks. It tells you what is wrong with it, what he
would cut, and hands back the marked-up version plus one thing to do this week.

It is a method, not a character. It will not talk to you as him and it will not
put words in his mouth.

---

## Install it

Nothing to do here. This tool installed with the rest of them in one step.
The classroom lesson **Install the tools** covers it once, in about three minutes.

## Use it

Start a message with any of these:

- `steve jobs` followed by what you want looked at
- `run jobs on this`
- `what would Jobs cut`
- `jobs review`
- `cold read this`
- `cut to the gems`

Then paste the actual thing. Not a description of it. The page, the list, the
price, the script.

**It will ask you one question first.** It writes back what it thinks is true
about your situation and waits for you to confirm. Answer it honestly, including
the parts that make you look unprepared. The whole output is built on that
premise, so a wrong premise gets you a confident wrong answer.

### The four procedures

It picks one based on what you brought.

| What you bring | What it runs |
|---|---|
| An idea or plan you have never built | The Demo Review |
| Too many offers, projects, audiences, features | Cut to the Gems |
| A landing page, signup flow, pricing page, onboarding | The Cold Read |
| A pitch, a sales call, a launch, a headline | The Road-Map Pitch |

## A worked example

**What you type:**

> steve jobs — cold read this. It's my landing page for a service where I edit
> podcasts for coaches. Headline is "Podcast editing that actually gets you
> heard." Below that: fast turnaround, custom intros, show notes, audiogram
> clips, transcript, and guest booking help. $400/mo. I haven't had a customer
> yet.

**What comes back, in shape:**

First it states your situation back and asks you to confirm it. Something like:
you sell done-for-you podcast editing to coaches, you have no customers yet, no
audience, you are working alone, and you brought a live landing page. Is this
right?

You say yes. Then it runs, and returns the block:

```
SITUATION
Pre-revenue podcast editing service for coaches. Live page,
no customers, no audience, solo, cash constrained.

PROCEDURE RUN
The Cold Read. You brought the thing a stranger meets
without you standing next to it.

WHAT IS WRONG WITH IT
1. Six offers on one page. Nobody can hold six things.
   The library's demonstration is one crumpled sheet
   caught and five thrown at once caught none.
   (Insanely Simple, Ken Segall)
2. The headline is a differentiation claim that is
   merely different, not measurably better. Everyone
   editing podcasts says they get you heard.
   (Jony Ive, Leander Kahney)
3. There is no named customer. You are writing to
   "coaches." The standard has nothing to bite on
   until it is one person having a bad afternoon.
   (Creative Selection, Ken Kocienda)
4. The page sells the product, not the improvement.
   (The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs, Carmine Gallo)

WHAT HE WOULD CUT
Five of the six line items. Guest booking goes first —
it is a different business. Keep the one thing that is
measurably better and say the number.

THE ARTIFACT
[your headline, rewritten]
[the five cut lines struck through, one survivor kept]
[the stranger's own description of your page, next to
what you meant it to say, with the gap named]
[time to first value, measured, and where they stalled]

DO THIS WEEK
Sit three people who have never seen your page in front
of it, say nothing, and time how long until they can
tell you what you sell and what it costs.

PROVES ME WRONG IF
All three read it cold, name your offer correctly inside
twenty seconds, and none of them ask what the six items
mean. Then the page is not the problem and your list is.

WHERE THE LIBRARY STOPS
Nothing in the source material covers landing page
conversion mechanics, ad platforms, or SEO. The
principles above are about comprehension, not traffic.
```

It will not tell you the page is a good start. That is on purpose.

## How this is sourced

An honest note, because it changes how you should read the output.

This skill reasons only from documented material about Steve Jobs held in one
research library. Books by Ken Segall, Carmine Gallo, Ken Kocienda, Leander
Kahney, Geoffrey Cain, Brent Schlender and Rick Tetzeli, the Steve Jobs Archive
collection edited by Leslie Berlin, his 1985 Playboy interview, and a recorded
conversation with Ed Catmull. Those books are named in the output when a claim
leans on one.

**It paraphrases. It does not quote him.** Almost all of that material is other
people writing about him, not him on the record. So every idea comes back in
plain words rather than in a line with quotation marks around it. If you ever
see it quote Steve Jobs, something has gone wrong — tell whoever gave you the
skill.

**It has holes, and it will name them.** A lot of the famous Jobs material is
not in this library. The Super Bowl ad, Xerox PARC, the calligraphy class, Reed
College, the reality distortion field, the ten-priorities exercise, his illness,
the turtleneck. Ask about any of those and it will tell you it cannot cover it
rather than making something up. That refusal is the feature. A tool that
invents a plausible Jobs anecdote is worse than no tool.

**It will not flatter you.** It is built to name the defect and the cut. If you
want to be told the thing is promising, this is the wrong tool.
