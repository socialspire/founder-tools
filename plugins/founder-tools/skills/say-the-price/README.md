# Say The Price

You already have a number in your head. You have probably had it for weeks.

The problem is not that you do not know what to charge. It is that you have never
said it out loud to someone who could say no, so the number keeps moving. And when
someone finally asks for a discount, you find out you never really decided.

This tool decides. You go in with a fuzzy number and a knot in your stomach. You
come out with three things and nothing else:

1. **One number.** Not a range. Not a starting point.
2. **The exact sentence** you say to state it.
3. **The rule for the first no**, written as a message you can actually send.

Takes about twenty minutes.

---

## What it will not do

It will not give you a range. It will not give you good, better and best. It will
not tell you it depends.

And it will refuse to price anything if you cannot say what the buyer actually
gains. If you tell it what you build instead of what changes for the person paying,
it stops and sends you to go have that conversation first. That refusal is not the
tool being difficult. Pricing a value nobody can name is how you end up with a
number you cannot defend.

It is a decision framework, not financial advice. The number is yours.

## Before you start

Open `worksheet.md` and fill it in. Ten minutes. You can also work through it live
in the chat, but you will answer better on paper than on the spot.

The one question that matters most is number 3: what the buyer gains, in their
words. If you have to guess at that one, go ask three real buyers before you run
the session. It is the difference between a number and a hope.

## What a session looks like

Here is one, start to finish. The founder is made up. The method is the real one.

### Maya

Maya cleans up the books for solo trades contractors. Electricians, plumbers, one
roofer. Three clients, all $150 a month.

She has never named a price in her life. The first client asked if $150 would work
and she said yes. The other two came through him and inherited the number.

**The tool asked what her buyer gains.** Her first answer was "monthly bookkeeping
and reconciliation." That is a deliverable, not a gain. It asked again.

Second answer: her roofer client stops doing his books on Sunday nights, and his
accountant stops charging him to untangle a year of receipts every March.

**Then the one number that proves it.** She had it, because he had told her. He was
paying his accountant $200 a month on retainer and still spending about six hours a
month on it himself. He bills at $85 an hour. That is roughly $710 a month the
problem is costing him right now.

**Then what it costs her.** Four hours per client per month. She named $50 an hour
for her time, which she had never said out loud before. Plus $20 of software. Her
floor is $220.

**Then the school.** Three tests:

- *Structural cost advantage?* No. She works faster than the roofer does, but
  nothing about how she delivers is hard to copy. That ruled out competing on being
  cheap.
- *Can the buyer verify her quality before paying?* Not really. He would have to
  take her word for it or call a reference. That ruled out charging a premium on
  quality alone, because premium without proof is just a higher number with a story
  on it.
- *Would losing one client break her?* With three clients, yes.

So: one flat published number, the same for everyone, no custom quotes.

**Then the number.**

Her floor is $220. The problem is costing her buyer about $710 a month. She needs
the business to produce $3,200 a month.

At $400 a month she keeps $380 per client after software, so she needs nine
clients. Nine clients is thirty-six hours of work a month. She knows more than nine
solo contractors. That count is findable.

At $150 she keeps $130, so she needs twenty-five clients for the same $3,200. That
is a hundred hours a month. And $150 is $70 under her own floor of $220, which
means she would be paying herself $32.50 an hour, not the $50 she just named out
loud.

Here is what she left with.

---

**THE NUMBER**

$400 per month. Published on her site. Same number for everyone.

That is up from the $150 she inherited. Nearly triple. Her buyer's problem
costs him about $710 a month today, so his gain is visibly larger than the price,
which is the test that matters.

**THE SENTENCE**

> Your books are reconciled and ready for your accountant by the 5th of every
> month, and you stop doing them on Sunday nights. It's $400 a month. That covers
> four hours on your accounts and the software that runs the reconciliation.

Then stop talking. Do not add "if that works for you." Do not add "but I'm
flexible." The sentence ends and you wait.

**THE HOLD**

First discount ask, send this and nothing else:

> Happy to talk about it. Too expensive compared to what?
>
> If there's another number in your head, I want to know what it's sitting next to.

If he names a comparison:

> That makes sense. What he's doing is a cleanup every March. What I'm doing is
> keeping it clean all year, which is what stops the six hours a month and the
> March bill.
>
> The price is $400 a month.
>
> If that's more than you need right now, I can do quarterly for $200 a month.
> That's a different job, not a discount on this one.

If he just says it's a lot of money:

> It is. Here's what's behind it: four hours a month on your accounts, plus the
> software.
>
> The price is $400 a month. If the timing's wrong, tell me when to come back.

Walk away when:

1. He wants her to work under someone else's name.
2. The only way to hit his number is to skip the reconciliation.
3. He would be more than 40% of her revenue, which is the ceiling she set herself.

Walk-away message:

> I don't think we're a fit at a number I can do this properly at. No hard feelings.
> If the March bill comes in worse than you expect, come back to me.

**School applied:** A. Flat and public, because losing one of three clients would
break her.

**Flag: UNTESTED.** She has agreed to a number. She has never stated one. So the
instruction is not to hold the price. It is to say $400 out loud to three real
contractors this week before she changes a single digit of it, and bring back what
they said.

The hold script still got written, because it has to exist before she needs it, not
during.

## One thing worth knowing before you run it

Nobody in the research library ever won by lowering a price because a prospect
pushed back.

Some of them priced low on purpose, from a cost advantage they had built first.
Some priced high and defended it. Every one of them decided the number before the
conversation, not during it.

That is the only thing this tool is really doing. Getting you to decide before,
instead of during.
