# Henry Ford — a skill you install

This runs Henry Ford's documented method on your actual work.

You paste in a price, an offer that has grown to six items, a roadmap you keep
reshuffling, or a pitch that is not landing. It tells you what is wrong with it,
what comes out, and hands back the marked-up version plus one thing to do this
week.

It is a method, not a character. It will not talk to you as him and it will not
put words in his mouth.

**One thing you should know before you install it.** Ford held documented
antisemitic views. That is a serious moral failing. The research library this
skill reads from states it in one line, before any method, and extracts only his
business method. The skill carries that line forward and will confirm it plainly
if you ask. It has nothing further to say about it, because the library does not
carry the detail and the skill is not allowed to invent any.

---

## Install it

You install this one on its own, from its course in the classroom. Two minutes.
It does not touch any of the other four, and you can add them later or not at all.

## Use it

Start a message with any of these:

- `henry ford` followed by what you want looked at
- `run Ford on this`
- `price-first`
- `tear down my costs`
- `what should I delete`
- `what is my service statement`

Then paste the actual thing. Not a description of it. The price, the offer, the
list, the pitch.

**It will ask you one question first.** It writes back what it thinks is true
about your situation and waits for you to confirm. Answer it honestly, including
the parts that make you look unprepared. The whole output is built on that
premise, so a wrong premise gets you a confident wrong answer.

### The four procedures

It picks one based on what you brought.

| What you bring | What it runs |
|---|---|
| A price, a quote, a margin question | The Price-First Cost Teardown |
| A feature list, an offer with several items, an onboarding flow | The Deletion Pass |
| A roadmap decision, a stalled list, competing priorities | The Service Statement Test |
| An unproven idea, a pitch that is not landing, no customers yet | The Incumbent Burden Total |

If you have never written down what your product actually does for a named
person, it runs the Service Statement Test first no matter what you asked for.
The other three test against that sentence, so without it they pass everything.

## A worked example

**What you type:**

> henry ford — tear down my pricing. I do done-for-you Notion setups for
> freelance designers. $600 flat. Takes me about 14 hours. I have had two paying
> clients. Nobody else is biting at that price.

**What comes back, in shape:**

First it states your situation back and asks you to confirm it. Something like:
you sell a done-for-you Notion build to freelance designers, two people have
paid, it is fourteen hours of your time per build, you are solo, and you brought
a price nobody new is accepting. Is this right?

You say yes. Then it runs, and returns the teardown sheet plus the block:

```
PROCEDURE: The Price-First Cost Teardown. You brought a price
and you have a real delivery behind it, so there is a cost
structure to force down rather than a guess.

WHAT IS WRONG WITH THIS
1. Your price is a guess dressed as a number. You set $600
   and then hoped the hours would fit under it.
2. Fourteen hours at any rate you would pay someone else
   puts your real unit cost above what you charge.
3. You have never asked what the build would have to stop
   including for the price to fall.

WHAT COMES OUT
Target price $360. To survive that, these go: the custom
dashboard nobody asked for, the two revision rounds, and
the kickoff call that could be a form. Roughly six hours.
Cost comes out of waste, not out of the build itself.

STANDS ON
Set the price first, then force the cost down to meet it.
No cost is fixed. From My Life and Work, Henry Ford.

DO THIS WEEK
Time your next build end to end, step by step, on Tuesday.
You cannot delete steps you have not measured.

WRONG IF
Your two clients tell you the dashboard and the revisions
are the reason they paid. Then those are the service, not
the waste, and the price is not the problem.

WHERE THE LIBRARY STOPS
The library has nothing on services, software, or pricing
anything that is not manufactured. It has nothing on
freelance markets or what designers pay. The loop applies.
The examples are all cars.
```

It will not tell you $600 is a fair price for good work. That is on purpose.

## How this is sourced

An honest note, because it changes how you should read the output.

This skill reasons only from documented material about Henry Ford held in one
research library, drawn from one book: *My Life and Work*, published in 1922.
That book is named in the output when a claim leans on it.

**One book is thinner than the companion skills.** The Musk and Jobs skills read
four to ten sources each. This one reads a single library entry. It will run out
of material sooner, on more subjects, and closer to the middle of what you think
you already know about him. That is why it is built to say the library does not
reach a question quickly rather than reaching for something plausible.

**It paraphrases. It does not quote him.** The material is a summary of a book,
not a transcript, and some lines in it may be the summariser's phrasing rather
than Ford's. So every idea comes back in plain words rather than in a line with
quotation marks around it. If you ever see it quote Henry Ford, something has
gone wrong — tell whoever gave you the skill.

**It has holes, and it will name them.** Most of the famous Ford material is not
in this library. The assembly line and how it worked. Any wage figure at all.
Every model except the Model T. The River Rouge plant. The name of the railroad
he bought. Both world wars. The engineering of the car. Dealers and
distribution. Any revenue or profit figure. Ask about any of those and it will
tell you it cannot cover it rather than making something up. That refusal is the
feature. A tool that invents a plausible Ford anecdote is worse than no tool.

**It marks what does not reach you.** Buying the supplier that blocks you.
Setting wages as a lever. Buying investors out. A twenty-year manufacturing
advantage. Improvements measured to the thousandth of a cent. None of those
shrink down to a founder with no customers, and the skill is built to say so and
give you the fragment that does transfer, rather than inventing a tiny version.

**It will not flatter you.** It is built to name the defect and the cut. If you
want to be told the thing is promising, this is the wrong tool.
