# Precedent Bank: The Proof Bench

Nineteen cards, every one traced to a named file in
`socialspire/skool/insights/`. Each card is one claim nobody had to believe and
one specific thing a person did about it — in most cases to make it checkable by
a stranger, in one case to judge whether someone else had.

**Why a bank and not a deck.** Facts sell and adjectives do not is knowledge
any strong model already holds, so a deck of that adds nothing. Nineteen
specific, verified cases — a goldfish bowl on a windowsill, a shovel of walnut
shells, forty-seven piston rings sent back out of fifty — is information. Read
the index, route to the sections the claim in front of you needs, and read only
those. Do not read the whole file on every run.

**Attribution rule.** Every card is paraphrase, including the ones marked
PODCAST. Not one phrase is quoted and none may be quoted downstream. Book cards
name the book and author. PODCAST cards name the founder, the show and the
date. Neither kind ever goes inside quotation marks, because the card text is
this bank's rendering, not the founder's words. If a member asks for a quotable
line, the bank holds none.

# A. Everyone in the category says the same thing

**A1. Robert Caro and the adjective he refused to use.** Situation: needing a
reader to conclude a man was ambitious without ever being told he was. Did:
threw out the adjective and used one thing a witness had actually seen — a
young man running uphill to work every morning, in winter and still in warm
weather. His stated rule for the whole method is to put the facts somewhere the
reader can see them, at which point explanation becomes unnecessary. Happened:
it is the technique behind books that each took eight to ten years and beat
every rival biographer working the same subjects. Then vs now: he had a decade
per book to find each detail and a founder has a week, so the sourcing cost
does not transfer. The swap does — one observed act outranks any quality word,
and the observation can be from last Tuesday. Move: take the strongest
adjective on your page, find one thing a real customer actually did that you or
someone else watched happen, and put that in its place.
Source: *Working*, Robert A. Caro.
`founders/2024-03-23-robert-caro-on-power-poverty-ruthlessness-obsession-running.md`

**A2. Les Schwab and the retread plant.** Situation: a tire dealer with nothing
to say about retreads that every competitor could not also say. Did: went to the
plant where his own product was physically made, watched them blend the retread
rubber, noticed the blend took either walnut shells or sawdust, and asked for
one shovel of each so he could sell the two separately — walnut for ice,
sawdust for snow. Happened: customers brought their old tires in specifically to
get it, and the line carried his advertising for years. Then vs now: a service
business has no blending floor, so the physical half does not transfer. The act
that does is going to where the work is actually done and naming a variable
nobody has bothered to mention. The fact already existed and nobody had looked
at it. Move: spend one hour this week inside your own delivery process, watching
it rather than running it, and write down one variable you have never once
mentioned to a customer.
Source: *Les Schwab: Pride In Performance — Keep It Going!*, Les Schwab.
`founders/2024-04-17-les-schwab-charlie-munger-recommended-this-book.md`

**A3. Steve Stoute and the founder of Reebok.** PODCAST. Situation: a challenger
paying for positioning against a leader who owned the only claim the category
cared about. Did: told the founder to his face that he could never convince a
sixteen-year-old that Reebok would make him jump higher than Nike, conceded the
performance claim publicly, and rebuilt the whole position on the claim he could
actually support — that people bought sneakers to match a hat and a shirt. He
then signed people who gave off no athletic energy at all, starting with Jay-Z.
Happened: it changed the company's trajectory and, by his account, reopened the
performance business to them later. Then vs now: he had a client budget to buy
attention for the replacement claim, which a bootstrapped founder does not. The
deletion costs nothing and is the whole move; the spend is optional and came
second. Move: name the one claim on your page that your category leader owns
outright, delete it rather than trying to out-argue it, and write the true claim
they are ignoring in its place.
Source: Steve Stoute, on the David Senra podcast, 2026-06-21.
`david-senra/2026-06-21-the-marketing-genius-of-steve-stoute.md`

**A4. Juan Trippe and the mountain road.** Situation: selling air freight to
buyers who already had a working alternative and no reason to change. Did:
refused to sell the service and sold the arithmetic instead — three days by
dangerous mountain road becoming ninety minutes by air, a forty-eight-fold gain.
Happened: the library holds it up as the model for how the strongest business
sellers lead, with saved time as the actual product being bought. Then vs now:
the ratio is only honest if both sides were timed. The corpus records the frame
and gives no method whatsoever for running the measurement, so a founder who has
not timed the customer's current way has an assertion dressed as a number. Move:
time the customer's current way once with a stopwatch, time yours once, and lead
with the multiple instead of the feature — and if you have not timed both, leave
the number out.
Source: *The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Carmine Gallo.
`founders/2024-07-03-how-to-sell-like-steve-jobs.md`

# B. The advantage is real and the buyer cannot see it

**B1. Henry Royce and the glass of water.** Situation: a car whose central claim
— no vibration — is the hardest kind of claim to assert credibly. Did: set a
full glass of water on the running car at 1,600 rpm and let people watch it.
Happened: not a drop spilled, and car advertising was still copying the device a
century later. He also earned the harder version: a car came back in 1923 in
perfect running order after 100,000 miles over Scottish hills. Then vs now: it
works because the test is unfakeable and reads in five seconds, so the audience
does not have to trust whoever is running it. That property is what transfers,
not the water. A demonstration the buyer has to be told how to interpret is not
this. Move: write down the one claim your whole price rests on, then design the
single object or reading that settles it in five seconds without a word of
explanation from you.
Source: *Rolls-Royce: The Magic of a Name*, Peter Pugh.
`founders/2024-02-07-the-biography-of-the-founder-of-rolls-royce.md`

**B2. Edwin Land and American Optical.** Situation: selling polarised lenses to
a buyer who would first have to be told what glare reduction even meant. Did:
rented a hotel room facing the sun, set a goldfish bowl on the windowsill so the
glare washed the view out, apologised to his guests that they probably could not
see the fish, and handed each of them a filter. Happened: he closed on the spot.
Then vs now: the room is the entire trick and it cost almost nothing, but it
requires a physical or on-screen condition you can stage in front of them. The
library has nothing at all on doing this over a video call. Move: build the
condition in which the buyer's current way visibly fails, put them inside it,
then hand them the fix rather than describing it — and make sure they are the
one who looks.
Source: *The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Carmine Gallo.
`founders/2024-07-03-how-to-sell-like-steve-jobs.md`

**B3. Andre and Edouard Michelin and the nails.** Situation: their one
structural advantage was that their tyre took minutes to change where a rival's
took most of a day, and no advertisement could make an audience feel that. Did:
staged their own race, required their tyres to enter, and seeded the entire
course with nails so the advantage had to show up. When the favourite was locked
to a competitor, they coaxed a retired champion out of retirement over a long
lunch. Happened: he finished more than seven hours ahead of the favourite. Then
vs now: engineering the venue is honest only when the advantage is genuinely
structural. Run the same play on a soft advantage and you have taught a room
full of prospects, at your own expense, that you are the same as everyone else.
Hosting your own contest also reads as marketing now in a way it did not in
1895. Move: name the condition under which your advantage becomes unmissable,
then build the demonstration so that condition is guaranteed to occur rather
than hoping it comes up.
Source: *The Michelin Men: Driving an Empire*, Herbert Lottman, and *Michelin: A
Century of Secrets*, Alain Germain.
`founders/2025-07-09-the-greatest-marketer-of-all-time-and-the-cofounder-of-the-michelin-family-dynas.md`

# C. You are doing all the talking

**C1. Elon Musk and Daimler.** Situation: Daimler asked Tesla for a proposal.
Did: skipped the proposal, built a working electric Smart car, and handed over
the keys — 0 to 60 in four seconds. Happened: Daimler invested $50M, which Musk
says saved the company. Then vs now: he had an engineering team and a car to
convert. A service founder's equivalent is doing one real unit of the actual
work unasked, which is slower per prospect and does not scale to a list. The
rule holds either way: they drove it themselves instead of reading about it.
Move: replace your next proposal with the crudest working version of the thing
and put the controls in the buyer's hands, then say nothing while they use it.
Source: *Elon Musk*, Walter Isaacson.
`founders/2025-08-26-how-elon-works.md`

**C2. Steve Jobs at NeXT and the founder who brought nothing.** Situation: a
company-defining deal contested by two founders in the same room. Did: brought
his engineering lead and a live demonstration, then handed the floor to his head
of software engineering rather than performing it himself. The competing founder
brought no team, no laptop and no presentation. Happened: Jobs won the deal.
Then vs now: this is Jobs at his least mythologised — the same file records him
burning $39M against a $3M plan and losing a $30M IBM deal over a slide problem
— so the case carries no halo and the win is attributable to the demo, not to
him. The half that transfers is that the person who built it ran it. Move: bring
the builder to your next important meeting and let them drive the demonstration
while you stand beside them, not in front of them.
Source: *Steve Jobs in Exile*, Geoffrey Cain.
`founders/2026-06-03-steve-jobs-in-exile.md`

**C3. Gaston Glock and the agencies.** Situation: an unknown manufacturer
needing institutional buyers whose choices the far larger civilian market
copied. Did: agencies asked for free units by the thousand in a single year, and
he shipped them with an invitation to simply send a check at a steep discount if
they wanted to keep them. Happened: nine in ten sent the check. Then vs now:
this is a sampling result, not a demonstration result — the file does not say
what any demo did to his close rate, and shipping thousands of free physical
units needed a 65 to 70 percent gross margin behind it. The transferable part is
that the trial and the decision both happened in the buyer's hands, on the
buyer's timetable, with a keep-or-return choice they made themselves. Move: put
working units in front of the buyers your real market watches, structure it as a
keep-or-return decision the buyer makes, and count the returns as your number.
Source: *Glock: The Rise of America's Gun*, Paul Barrett.
`founders/2024-03-26-the-biography-of-the-founder-of-glock.md`

**C4. Joseph Duveen and John D. Rockefeller Jr.** Situation: a buyer who said a
million and a half was too high. Did: refused to move the price and moved the
clock instead — left the pieces in Rockefeller's house for a year on option,
with a hard expiry date. Happened: Rockefeller paid the full price the day
before the option ran out. Then vs now: possession does the persuading and the
deadline converts it, but the corpus records this on physical objects only, and
Duveen's wider conduct includes paying a client's butler for information, which
is not to be copied. The trial has to actually end on the date or the mechanism
is gone. Move: when a prospect says the price is too high, do not discount —
offer an unpaid trial at the full price with a dated end, and hold the date.
Source: *The Days of Duveen*, S.N. Behrman.
`founders/2024-04-26-the-robber-barons-art-dealer-joseph-duveen.md`

# D. The buyer asks who else says this

**D1. Lulu Cheng Meservey and the bought launch.** PODCAST. Situation: judging
from the outside whether a company's public proof is real. Did: named two tells
— the identical cinematic launch video everyone now makes, and engagement wildly
out of proportion to how interesting the thing actually is. Her example was a
post carrying a few hundred likes and roughly 4,700 retweets, where the
retweeting accounts had no history except shilling. Happened: her response is
total and permanent — she writes off the entire company as fake, will never buy
from it, and will never recommend anyone work there. Then vs now: this is a 2026
judgement about 2026 signals, so it is the most current case here and the most
likely to date. The rule under it is older than the platforms: proof that could
have been purchased is discounted to zero by the exact people whose opinion you
needed, and the penalty falls on the whole company, not the one post. Move: go
through every proof point on your page and ask of each whether it could have
been bought; delete the ones that could, even the true ones, unless you can show
how they were produced.
Source: Lulu Cheng Meservey, on the David Senra podcast, 2026-08-12.
`david-senra/2026-08-12-the-comms-strategist-behind-anduril-shopify-cognition-lulu-cheng-meservey.md`

**D2. Warren Buffett and the sellers he had already bought from.** Situation:
asking a founder to sell a lifetime's work on promises that every rival bidder
was also making. Did: enclosed a list of every person Berkshire had ever bought
a business from and invited the reader to contact any of them and check the
performance against the promises. Happened: it positioned Berkshire as an
unusual third option beside the operator who eventually imposes its own managers
and the financier who resells and dresses up the accounting. Then vs now: the
completeness is the whole mechanism. A curated list is a testimonial page and
every buyer knows how those are made, so a partial version of this move is
weaker than not making it. Nothing in the corpus records what happens when
someone on the list says something unflattering. Move: publish the complete
reference list, including the customers you would rather leave off, and tell the
prospect to pick whoever they want and call them without you on the line.
Source: *The Essays of Warren Buffett*, Warren Buffett — appended segment.
`founders/2024-02-16-the-biography-of-bill-gates.md`

**D3. Yvon Chouinard and his own customers.** Situation: staged model
photography that looked false to the exact community it was aimed at. Did: put a
single line in the mail-order catalogue asking real customers to send in
photographs of real people doing real things. Happened: submissions flooded in,
free and plentiful, and became the visual proof the brand ran on. Then vs now:
he had a catalogue reaching a community that already identified with him, so the
volume does not transfer to a founder with fifty customers. Fifty photographs is
still fifty pieces of evidence produced by someone other than you, which is the
only property that matters here. Move: ask your existing customers for their own
material this week instead of commissioning a polished asset, and publish what
arrives unretouched.
Source: *Let My People Go Surfing*, Yvon Chouinard.
`founders/2024-03-08-yvon-chouinard-founder-of-patagonia.md`

**D4. Ettore Bugatti and the catalogue.** Situation: a car priced above every
competitor of equal horsepower, justified only by the assertion that it was in a
class by itself. Did: opened every annual catalogue with the list of races won,
and kept every racing model a catalogue model any customer could actually buy.
Happened: the racetrack served as both his testing bench and his entire
distribution channel — win the race and buyers lined up. Then vs now: most
modern service categories have no public ranked contest, and hosting your own is
not the same thing. Where a real one exists the mechanism is exact, and the
second half matters more than the first: the thing that won and the thing you
sell have to be the same object, or the proof is about a version nobody can buy.
Move: find the one public, ranked test your buyer already reads, enter the exact
configuration you actually sell rather than a special build, and print the result
at the top of the page.
Source: *The Bugatti Story*, L'Ebe Bugatti.
`founders/2024-04-03-the-biography-of-bugatti.md`

# E. The claim will never have a document behind it

**E1. Ivar Kreuger and the completion date.** Situation: construction clients
carried the entire cost of a delay and had no power to prevent it, while the
builder had all the power and no exposure. Did: became the first firm in Europe
to commit to a fixed completion date, pay a penalty for every day late, and
collect a bonus for every day early. Happened: the firm won the bonus on every
project and became the top firm in Sweden within a few years, because buyers
valued a guaranteed date enough to pay extra for it. Then vs now: Kreuger later
ran one of the largest frauds of the century, so the man is not the model and
this predates all of it. The mechanism is the cleanest route in the corpus from
an assertion to a fact: you stop claiming reliability and start being
financially exposed to it, which is checkable by the customer without any third
party at all. Move: take the claim you cannot document, attach a penalty you
would genuinely pay, and charge for the guarantee instead of discounting to win
the deal.
Source: *The Match King*, Frank Partnoy.
`founders/2024-05-15-the-financial-genius-behind-a-century-of-wall-street-scandals-ivar-kreuger.md`

**E2. Ludwig Nobel and the Baku oil town.** Situation: an industry every buyer
assumed was crooked, where an honesty claim is worthless because everyone makes
it. Did: publicly offered to face inquiry and to make amends if anyone could
prove his company had cheated — a standing, open invitation to be investigated.
Happened: the file records the offer as the differentiator in a dishonest trade,
and the family name became something workers wore as a badge because it meant
they were the best in Russia. Then vs now: no number is attached and the corpus
does not record anyone taking him up on it, so treat the outcome as unmeasured.
What transfers is the structure: an offer to be checked is only proof if the
remedy you attach would actually hurt you to pay. Move: publish the guarantee
your competitors would be afraid to make, name the remedy in advance, and set
the remedy high enough that offering it is itself the evidence.
Source: *The Russian Rockefellers*, Robert Tolf.
`founders/2024-08-19-the-nobel-family-dynasty.md`

# F. The verdict could go against you

**F1. Hans Wilsdorf and the Channel swimmer.** Situation: a waterproof-case
claim nobody would accept on the maker's own word. Did: put an Oyster around the
neck of a swimmer attempting the English Channel in freezing November water,
then bought the entire front page of the Daily Mail on 27 November 1927 to
publish the result. Happened: she failed the crossing and the watch ran
perfectly for ten hours. The verdict on the swim went against the swimmer and
the verdict on the watch still landed, because the claim under test was never
that she would finish. Then vs now: buying a national front page is not
available to a founder and the amplification was half the play. The half that
transfers is free — define in advance exactly what the outside event proves, so
that the third party's own failure cannot take your claim down with it. Move:
before you attach your product to anyone else's attempt, write the sentence you
will publish if they fail, and if that sentence is not still true and still
worth publishing, do not attach yourself.
Source: *Rolex Jubilee Vade Mecum*, Hans Wilsdorf.
`founders/2024-07-08-the-founder-of-rolex-hans-wilsdorf.md`

**F2. Soichiro Honda and Toyota.** Situation: a first real order from the buyer
whose judgement effectively defined the industry. Did: shipped piston rings and
let Toyota inspect them properly rather than pre-selling the quality. Happened:
Toyota returned 47 of the first 50. He aimed his engineering at automation
immediately afterwards rather than disputing the verdict. Then vs now: a verdict
this brutal is only available where a buyer will actually inspect and report
back; most modern buyers just stop replying, which gives you nothing to work
from. He also used races as what he called floating research — entered for what
they taught, not for the trophy — which is the same posture applied on purpose.
Move: ask your most demanding buyer to reject your work in writing and to say
why, then treat the rejection rate as your measurement rather than as an insult.
Source: *Honda: The Man and His Machines*, Sol Sanders.
`founders/2026-06-28-the-practical-genius-of-honda.md`

# G. What is not on record

Every one of these was searched for and not found. State the relevant one in
every run. It is a required output line, because a corpus that has been searched
can say what it does not contain, and nothing reasoning from memory can. Where
an item names the closest thing that does exist, the file that near miss comes
from is named with it, in the same form the cards use.

**How to state it.** As an absence, never as a verdict. Holding no case of a
demonstration run over a video call does not mean video demonstrations fail. It
means nothing is on record either way, so the member should price the attempt as
untested and instrument it themselves.

**The words the member reads.** Open by saying you have no documented case of
it, name their own situation in plain words, then name the closest case and how
it differs. The member never reads the words library, bank, card or corpus of
this file, and never a flourish about being the first. An absence is
information, not a warning against the ruling.

1. **No demonstration of software, or of any service, conducted over a video
   call or screen share.** Every demonstration in the corpus is physical and in
   the same room — a glass of water, a goldfish bowl, a carousel, a set of car
   keys, a shelf of free units. Nothing on record tells a founder how the
   mechanism behaves when the buyer is on the other end of a screen. The
   carousel is the only one of those five with no card of its own.
   `founders/2025-07-09-the-greatest-marketer-of-all-time-and-the-cofounder-of-the-michelin-family-dynas.md`
2. **No case of a service, agency or consulting business building a
   demonstration at all.** Every demonstration on record involves an object a
   stranger can touch or drive. A founder selling hours has no precedent here
   and should treat the demo design as untested rather than as a proven form.
3. **No cost or duration figure for obtaining any third-party verdict.** Nobody
   in the corpus reports what a certification, test, benchmark or inspection
   cost them or how long it took. Wilsdorf's chronometer certificate and
   Bugatti's race entries arrive with no price and no lead time attached.
   `founders/2024-07-08-the-founder-of-rolex-hans-wilsdorf.md`
   `founders/2024-04-03-the-biography-of-bugatti.md`
4. **No case of a founder publishing a third-party result that went against
   them.** Honda's 47-of-50 rejection was a private buyer verdict he acted on,
   and Wilsdorf's swimmer failed at her own goal rather than the watch's. The
   corpus therefore cannot say what publishing an unfavourable verdict does to a
   business, in either direction.
   `founders/2026-06-28-the-practical-genius-of-honda.md`
   `founders/2024-07-08-the-founder-of-rolex-hans-wilsdorf.md`
5. **No conversion, close-rate or win-rate figure attached to any demonstration
   anywhere.** The nearest number is Glock's nine-in-ten payment on free
   samples, which measures a keep-or-return decision, not a demo. Any claim that
   a demonstration lifts close rate by some amount is invented.
   `founders/2024-03-26-the-biography-of-the-founder-of-glock.md`
6. **No case of a demonstration failing publicly, and nothing on what the
   founder did next.** Every staged demonstration in the corpus worked. There is
   no recorded recovery from a demo that went wrong in front of a buyer.
7. **No case of a named customer refusing to be used as a reference, and no
   record of what the founder did instead.** Buffett's complete seller list and
   Chouinard's customer photographs both arrive with everyone already saying
   yes.
   `founders/2024-02-16-the-biography-of-bill-gates.md`
   `founders/2024-03-08-yvon-chouinard-founder-of-patagonia.md`
8. **No before-and-after time or cost saving published by a named customer with
   a sample size next to it.** Turner built his own instrument and reported only
   the conclusion; nobody in the corpus publishes the measurement method
   alongside the number.
   `founders/2024-04-14-the-autobiography-of-ted-turner.md`
9. **No modern certification, audit, review platform or ratings body appears
   anywhere.** The library predates all of them. It can say that an outside
   verdict converted a claim into a fact in 1914; it cannot say which of today's
   badges a buyer actually weighs.
   `founders/2024-07-08-the-founder-of-rolex-hans-wilsdorf.md`
10. **No case of paying for a third-party verdict and having the payment
    disclosed, or of a buyer discovering the payment afterwards.** Michele
    Ferrero paid buyers to be interviewed behind one-way glass, but that was
    private research, not published proof.
    `founders/2025-06-23-michele-ferreros-40-billion-privately-owned-chocolate-empire.md`
